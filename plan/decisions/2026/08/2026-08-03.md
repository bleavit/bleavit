## 16 §8.6 — hosted slots are admitted at a descending price under contention (N14); one new registry row, `svc.price_cap`.
authorized_by: Chralt98 (2026-08-03 ruling: *"I would rather want to set the price dynamically according to the market demand than at fixed prices"*), under the 02 §13 sign-off the user owns
docs_touched: `13` §1 (`svc.price_cap` + note); `16` §8.6; `02` §9; `docs/integration/costs.md`; `tools/limit-coverage/registry.toml`

Implements the user's 2026-08-03 ruling. `svc.max_live` bounded *how many* questions run and said nothing about *who gets a slot* when demand exceeds it, so the answer was transaction latency. The rule: the §8.1 tariff is multiplied by a scarcity factor `M ≥ 1` that moves **up immediately on admission and down only gradually toward 1, including after a slot is freed** — so a released slot descends from the pre-release price rather than dropping to the post-release one, and immediacy is what a sniper pays for. There is **no starting-price parameter**: each descent begins from the running `M`, which is the record of prior demand. Forced rather than stylistic — hosted windows are client-chosen and free asynchronously, so there is no batch of identical units and no shared clock to open a sale on, which is the specific reason Polkadot's bulk-coretime shape does not port. **Quantity is never market-discovered**: `svc.max_live` stays a hard governance cap the mechanism cannot sell past, because a quantity-discovering controller makes scarcity directly profitable and TH-73 names that pressure; 15 §4.9's competing-venue leg is why the coretime analogy breaks — additional hosted depth degrades Bleavit's own decision formation, where additional cores do not degrade existing cores. Premium above the floor routes to POL at `Sealed` only (the fee is refunded on VOID), so the charge for congestion funds the repair for it. **Exactly one new row**, and the mechanism's other two quantities are derived from keys that already exist: decay is anchored to `svc.max_window` (a price cannot outlive the demand that set it) and the per-admission step is `svc.price_cap^(1/svc.max_live)` (taking every slot at once arrives exactly at the ceiling, never past it). **`svc.price_cap` ships `[VERIFY]`-unset because its natural anchor does not exist** — TH-72's attack-cost cell carries no figure (SQ-574), and an equalization against 08 §7's priced channels was attempted and **refused** as incommensurable: queue-flooding blocks *admission*, depth-thinning starves an *already-trading* proposal. **The unset consumer defaults to `M = 1`, not to a refusal, and that asymmetry against `svc.client_bond` is deliberate:** the bond prices registration abuse, so refusing is the safe direction; this row gates an *allocation refinement*, so `M = 1` reproduces today's behaviour while a refusal would close the service entirely and be strictly worse than the status quo it replaces. Fail-closed is a rule about the direction of error, not a reflex. **No `INTEGRATION_CONTRACT_VERSION` bump** — a PARAM row binding through `params()` is not a contract change (02 §9's own precedent), but 02 records the trap that an unset `svc.price_cap` means *operational at `M = 1`*, the opposite of what an unset `svc.fee_bps` meant. `docs/integration/costs.md` updated so clients size budgets from the quoted fee rather than the formula.

## 16 §8.7 — the cannibalization response becomes automatic and graduated (N15); no new registry row.
authorized_by: Claude, under R-1, implementing the user's 2026-08-03 ruling (*"consider the problem of slot sniping in milestones N14 and N15"*)
docs_touched: `16` §8.4, §8.7

16 §8.4's fourth mitigation ended in *"the values layer **MUST** reduce `svc.max_live`"* — the only response in the document that is a governance promise rather than a mechanism, asking for a vote precisely when revenue argues against one, on evidence that will always be arguable, in a system whose every other protection (the I-4 latch, PB-LEDGER-FREEZE, the dead-man switch) is automatic. **The response moves the price, not the quantity, and the quantity latch the milestone was named for was designed and then rejected twice.** A *binary* latch creates the behaviour it exists to prevent: operators watching contest capital approach the threshold register before it trips, so the cliff itself produces a burst of admissions at the worst moment. And *any* quantity latch grandfathers incumbents — stopping new registrations while live questions continue makes a slot-holder's position more valuable exactly when Bleavit is damaged, and 16 §8.4 prices damaging Bleavit's own books at round-trip fees, so suppressing Bleavit's contest capital would become profitable for an incumbent. A price response has no threshold to race and no exclusivity to win: the door stays open, the incumbent's own next question costs the same raised price, and `svc.max_live` still bounds the harm structurally. **Trigger on the input, not the output:** a `NotDecisionGrade` rejection caused by the hosted service is indistinguishable from one caused by nobody finding the proposal interesting, so the rejection count can never settle the argument however long it is collected — contest capital in Bleavit's own decision books is the quantity `dec.v_min` gates on, so it is measured directly. **Three implementation choices are load-bearing.** (i) The measure divides the 04 §7a contest integral by the blocks *actually accrued*, not by the full window width; the natural-looking `average_contest_at` divides by the full width, which reports every young window as starved and would fire the latch at the start of every epoch. (ii) The two halves of `M` combine by `max`, not by a product, so `M ≤ svc.price_cap` holds by construction — a product would reach `svc.price_cap²` and would need a second registry row to say what that bound means. (iii) The starvation reading is **never stored**, which forced N14's `raise_scarcity` to ratchet the *contention* term rather than the combined value: otherwise a single admission during a transient starvation would bake that level into stored state and decay it out over `svc.max_window`, hysteresis outliving its cause. **No new parameter (R-2 step 1).** The response magnitude reuses `svc.price_cap`, so one adopted row arms both halves of `M` and N15 is inert on exactly N14's condition; a second ceiling whose job the first already does would be a defect, not a parameter. **Stated honestly in the spec rather than smoothed over:** this is a Pigouvian charge and not a repair — it adds no depth to a starved Bleavit book, the revenue goes to `MAIN` like the rest of instrument D, and when no Bleavit decision book is live the probe reads healthy, so a client can register into a quiet gap and contend with windows that open afterwards (§8.4's scheduling refusal sees only proposals live *at registration*). **No `INTEGRATION_CONTRACT_VERSION` bump** — no 02 surface moved.

## Hosted-question slots are sold at a demand-responsive descending price, not first-come-first-served (N14), and the slot cap becomes a graduated latch rather than a governance promise (N15).
authorized_by: User, 2026-08-03, by explicit ruling (*"I would rather want to set the price dynamically according to the market demand than at fixed prices"*), after proposing the coretime mechanism and then identifying the velocity gap in my occupancy-only design
docs_touched: None yet — PLAN only; 16/13/08/14/15 amendments land with N14 and N15

User ruling, reached across three exchanges that each corrected the previous design, so the reasoning is recorded rather than just the outcome. **(1) The gap.** Today's admission is `LiveQuestionCount < max_live` — pure first-come-first-served at a posted two-part tariff. Under contention that allocates by transaction latency, not by value: sixteen mediocre questions can lock out an important one, and a freed slot is a bot race. 16 §736 already reasons about scarcity (*"pricing scarce capacity at marginal cost prices it at zero"*) but prices it only with a floor, which is scarcity-aware pricing without a scarcity-aware **allocator**. **(2) Why not Polkadot's coretime auction, which the user proposed.** Its descending price transfers; the rest does not. Coretime sells homogeneous cores on one synchronized 28-day clock, and hosted questions have client-chosen windows that free asynchronously, so there is no batch of identical units to auction. Its renewal right protects a parachain whose core loss is existential, whereas a question simply ends — and a renewal right here would let a client squat the service. Most importantly, **its price controller exists to drive utilization toward a target**, which is correct where selling more cores does not degrade the cores; N12 measured that selling more hosted slots *does* degrade Bleavit's own governance, so importing a quantity-discovering controller would point the feedback loop backwards and make scarcity profitable — the precise pressure TH-73 names. Adopted: adaptive price, hard governance-set quantity. **(3) The user then found the real hole in my first mechanism.** Occupancy pricing is a state function with no memory of velocity, so it cannot answer *"will the price rise if slots get acquired too quickly?"* — sixteen slots taken in one block price identically to sixteen taken over three weeks. A jump-on-purchase term alone does **not** fix it either: after a quiet period the term has decayed, so the first snipe of a freed slot is still cheap. What fixes it is an **asymmetry** — the price moves up instantly on purchase and down only gradually toward the floor, **including after a release**, so a freed slot descends from the pre-release level instead of dropping to the new one. That single rule is simultaneously the per-slot Dutch auction, the velocity term and the demand-discovery loop, and it **prices** sniping rather than preventing it (a bot valuing the slot above the premium still wins, which is the mechanism working). **There is deliberately no start-price parameter**: each descent begins from the running price, which is the accumulated record of prior demand — more responsive than coretime's once-per-period controller, and one stored value plus a lazy decay rather than a broker pallet. **(4) Derivation anchor, and the caveat that constrains it (R-2 step 2).** The N12 competing-venue curve measures decision-grade formation loss per unit of external occupancy, so the marginal slot can be priced at its marginal **measured** damage, valued through the existing `sec.prize.*` keys — a price that is a function of harm rather than a number someone liked, rising steeply exactly where the measurement says damage accelerates. But mapping slots to a diversion fraction runs through the proportional-to-depth flow model that 16 §8.4 now records as **anchored, not derived, with an unsafe error direction**, so the curve MUST be derived from the **0.75 stress rung, not the 0.50 arming rung**: over-pricing costs revenue, under-pricing costs decisions, and the hard cap bounds the unsafe direction regardless, which is what makes this shippable at all. **(5) Two gaming findings that change N15's shape**, both surfaced by carrying the sniping lens into the latch. A **binary** latch creates its own race — operators watching contest capital approach the threshold will register before the cliff — so the response must be graduated, with no cliff to race. And a latch that **grandfathers incumbents pays them to cause it**: locking out new entrants while live questions continue makes a slot-holder's position more valuable precisely when Bleavit is damaged, so suppressing Bleavit's own contest capital becomes profitable for an incumbent. Answered by raising the price for everyone rather than closing the door, with the increase routed to POL, so the incumbent windfall is taxed into the repair. N15 therefore becomes N14's curve with a second input rather than separate machinery. *(N15 riding N14's curve means it inherits N14's destination, which implementation moved to `MAIN` — see (6).)* **(6) Preserved invariants:** `svc.max_live` remains a hard cap the mechanism can never sell past; the floor keeps going to MAIN as cost recovery; the premium routes to POL only at `seal`, since the fee is refunded on VOID. *(That last clause was mine, not the user's ruling, and it did not survive implementation — the whole scaled fee goes to MAIN. See the 16 §8.6 row below and 16 §8.6 itself: a direct POL credit opens a second money path into protocol subsidy custody, and `MAIN` is what funds POL anyway.)* **No spec text amended by this row** — it records the decision and its derivation obligations; the 16, 13, 08, 14 and 15 amendments land with N14 and N15 themselves, and no parameter value is adopted here.

## The competing-venue block publishes no composite "ready to arm" boolean, and the first full run is what forced that.
authorized_by: Session decision under R-1 (delegated batch authority; evidence-driven, no 02 surface touched)
docs_touched: 15 §4.9; 16 §8.4

The leg originally emitted `arming_ready = security gate ∧ control agreement`. On the committed 10⁴ corpus that field came back **`True`** — beside decision-grade formation falling **39–84 %** at the same rung and a `resolution` block showing **every class `gate_is_all_or_nothing`**, i.e. unable to express any rate below 1 % at all (PARAM carries 12 decidable-harm proposals; its smallest non-zero rate is 8.3 %). So the boolean was True for two bad reasons at once: no false pass was *observable*, and none *could* be, while the harm the leg exists to detect was arriving as denial rather than corruption. A field named `arming_ready` is consumable by a future phase gate, and a phase gate that armed the hosted service on that evidence would be doing so on a screen it mistook for a measurement. **Removed, not renamed-and-kept**: the block now publishes `security_leg_clean` (what the gate actually establishes), `liveness_loss_at_arming` (the magnitude, per class), `security_leg_is_a_screen_not_a_measurement`, and a `no_single_verdict` note stating that composing the two would require a liveness threshold **no document names** — which R-2 forbids inventing. The artifact checker now **refuses** a block that reintroduces `arming_ready`, so the false comfort cannot return by accident. Two spec questions carry what this cannot settle: **SQ-571** (does D-20's own arming condition admit a chain state that guts Bleavit's governance? — the leg models diversion with no offsetting capital import, so it is a worst case, and saying which of three readings is right needs that import term) and **SQ-572** (is a screen the intended standard for a Phase-4 arming input, given full resolution costs ≈ 4× the primary run). **The general lesson, worth more than the field:** the leg was built to prevent exactly this misreading, its criterion string said so in words — and it still shipped a boolean that contradicted its own prose. Prose warnings do not protect machine consumers; only the absence of the field does.

## The competing-venue arming criterion has two halves, and only the security half can be a threshold (N12).
authorized_by: Session decision under R-1 (delegated batch authority; the user owns both sides of 02 §13's sign-off and no 02 surface is touched, so no contract bump)
docs_touched: 15 §4.9; 16 §8.4

16 §8.4 and 15 §4.9 gained the executable form of the "S4 re-run with a competing-venue term" obligation, and writing it changed what the criterion should be. **The arming rung is anchored, not derived — and my first version of this row claimed otherwise.** 16 §8.4's arming condition `Σ b_ext ≤ Σ pol.b(live)` bounds external **depth** at parity; converting a depth bound into a **flow** bound takes a behavioural model, and proportional-to-depth allocation is what puts the anchor at **one half**. An adversarial review refuted the stronger claim with a decisive counterexample: an external venue of *equal depth* but better fees or returns can take nearly all the flow, because depth parity does not imply flow parity. Worse, the first version's config **refused any rung above 0.50** as "a chain state §8.4 forbids" — backwards twice over, since a higher rung describes a different *flow model* on a perfectly admissible chain state, and refusing it suppressed the only probe of the direction in which the anchor is optimistic. Corrected: the ladder now **requires** a rung above the arming rung (0.25 / 0.50 / **0.75**), the artifact publishes a `stress_by_class` verdict beside `arming_by_class`, and where the two disagree the arming decision's dependence on proportional allocation is a published fact instead of a buried one. Diversion thins **organic** formation on decision, gate and Baseline books and leaves attacker budgets alone: an adversary targeting one proposal is not diverted by a venue elsewhere (14 TH-72), so the term thins the defense without thinning the attack. **The measurement then contradicted the obligation's own stated premise**, which is why this is a decision and not a mechanical implementation. The plan and 16 §8.4 both anticipated that diversion would make δ *under-sized* — thinner books, cheaper flips. What the simulation shows is that thinned books mostly stop being decision-grade at all (`NotDecisionGrade` 15 → 33 on a 40-proposal probe; CODE decision-grade formation 0.625 → 0.250), so the proposal is **rejected rather than flipped**. A false-pass-only criterion therefore reads *cleaner the more governance the diversion destroys* — and TH-72 already names the real harm this way: "proposals rejected for thin books, with no market signal that anything was attacked". The criterion is therefore split: **security gates** (per-class decidable-harm false-pass < 1 % at the arming rung, else δ rises before arming) and **liveness is measured against 16 §8.4's stated falsifier with no invented threshold** (R-2 — the doc states the falsifier and deliberately no number, so cutting `svc.max_live` stays a values decision). Both docs now say a clean security half alone MUST NOT be reported as a clean result. **Scoping:** the leg is a Phase-4 arming input and never enters `violations` — Phase 0 has no hosted service, and a Phase-4 condition cannot retro-invalidate a Phase-0 exit measured on a chain where it does not apply. Zero diversion is a strict no-op, asserted on executed evidence rather than on `x·1 == x`, so the published Phase-0 result is untouched
