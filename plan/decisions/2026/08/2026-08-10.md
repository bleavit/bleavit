# Decisions — 2026-08-10

## The trading-reward sovereign's derivation is frozen,…
authorized_by: Session, under R-1 (spec rulings delegated 2026-07-25; no D-n moves, no 02 surface moves). Items 2 and 3 raised by the TR7 review as spec gaps rather than defects
docs_touched: 08 §2.1, §2.6 (rules 3 and the funding paragraph), 13 §3.5

The trading-reward sovereign's derivation is frozen, and rule 3's fee treatment is stated rather than left to be inferred.

Three items, all raised by TR7 or its review. **(1) `PalletId(*b"bl/trwrd")`** now appears in 08 §2.1 and its 13 §3.5 mirror, as that section requires both copies to. The account is unusual in holding two assets at once — the participants' USDC bonds and the authorized VIT budget — and TR7 chose the seed rather than reading it from a document, which is exactly the kind of constant that ossifies unrecorded. It sits **outside** the R-4 endowment set: R-4 does not name it, and it needs no endowment because the first `enroll` creates it with a bond §2.6 floors at `ledger.pos_dep`, ten times the USDC `min_balance`. The reviewer checked the claim rather than accepting it — the USDC foreign asset is genesis-created `is_sufficient`, so the creating transfer needs no prior provider reference. **(2) Rule 3 credits gross of `ledger.redeem_fee`, deliberately**, where rule 2 credits net of `mkt.fee`. The asymmetry looks like the SQ-1048 defect and is not one: **no single "net" exists to credit.** 03 §5.3a gives the paired redemption path a different fee base from the single-leg path, and which path a trader takes is their later choice, so a net figure would require the score to predict a redemption that has not happened. The over-credit is bounded by one `ledger.redeem_fee` — 30 bps at the 13 §1 default — and is absorbed where the program already carries its safety margin: the adopted `rwd.rate` sits **2.4× inside** the wash break-even, so a 30 bps shift in realized proceeds moves that ratio in the third decimal. The annulled arm is unaffected, since 13 §1 records that the mirror leg exits through the fee-exempt `redeem`. **(3) The accrual reserve is valued at the sweep-time rate and each claim at the claim-time rate**, and §2.6 now says so. `usdc_to_vit` is inversely proportional to `fee.vit_usdc_rate`, so a downward amendment after a sweep leaves outstanding claims needing more VIT than was reserved, bounded by the 10× envelope. The consequence is **liveness, not solvency**: the transfer fails inside the storage layer, the accrual survives intact, and no new accrual can be made against the shortfall because the budget re-reads the balance at the same live rate. It is inherent to §2.6 defining the reserve as a VIT figure derived from a governed rate, not to the adapter that computes it

## `settled_value` is a fraction of par, not a whole…
authorized_by: Session, under R-1 (spec rulings delegated 2026-07-25; no D-n moves, no 02 surface moves). Raised by TR7 as a DONE_WITH_CONCERNS deviation from a type TR5 had frozen, in its own revertible commit
docs_touched: 08 §2.6 rule 3; `crates/trading-rewards-core/`, `pallets/trading-rewards/`

`settled_value` is a fraction of par, not a whole number — the fourth load-bearing defect, and the same shape as the third.

08 §2.6 rule 3 said `settled_value` is *"the branch's terminal redemption value per unit"*, which reads as an amount and was implemented as a `Balance` integer. It cannot be one. [03](../../../../docs/architecture/03-conditional-ledger.md)'s `redeem_scalar` pays `amount × s / SCORE_SCALE`, so a unit of a branch that settled at 0.6 is worth six tenths of a base unit and never a whole number of them. **Read as an integer, every sub-par settlement floors to zero:** `received` collects nothing and rule 4's realized arm folds a debit of the entire notional against a trader whose forecast was correct. That is exactly the defect SQ-1051's annulled arm was written to prevent, arriving through the **realized** arm instead — so the fix that closed one path left the other open, in a value nobody had thought to type-check. Rule 3's own *"rounded down"* was the tell: an integer product needs no rounding. Ruled: `settled_value` is on the ledger's own `SCORE_SCALE` grid and clamped to par, with an overflow-free product. Found by TR7 while writing the adapter that had to populate it — the first task that could not avoid the question, because no earlier task had to produce a real value

## The return of unspent reward budget is not…
authorized_by: Session, under R-1 (spec rulings delegated 2026-07-25; no D-n moves, no 02 surface moves). Found by the controller while triaging TR6's own disclosure that the call's origin and existence were its design rather than the spec's
docs_touched: 08 §2.6 (the funding paragraph gains the return mechanism)

The return of unspent reward budget is not permissionless, and 08 §2.6 now says so.

§2.6 stated the outcome — *"unspent budget returns to the pot at epoch close"* — and no mechanism, so TR6 reasonably built a public crank, modelled on §1.2's `reconcile_insurance`. **That is a one-extrinsic, permanent denial of the whole program's payout for an epoch.** Reward accrual is clamped to the budget's unpromised remainder; participants settle by pull after their epoch closes; and `settle_epoch` resets the score accumulator unconditionally and is idempotent per participant per epoch. So anyone could sweep the headroom mid-settlement for a transaction fee, and every participant who settled afterwards would close at a **zero** reward with their score discarded. Re-funding does not undo it — their epoch is already settled. The `reconcile_insurance` precedent does not transfer: reconciling harms nobody when it runs early, while sweeping destroys value still in flight. The implementer copied the shape without asking whether the precondition came with it, which is the same class as the plan's coincidental tests. **Ruled:** the return carries the **same `FutarchyParam` origin as the authorization**, and the natural shape folds it into `fund_trading_rewards`, so each authorization retires the previous remainder and no independent surface exists. That is not governance discretion in substance — the funding call is the only thing that can put budget there — but it puts the timing under the same authority as the spending rather than under whoever transacts first. §2.6 also now states the **amount**: the sovereign's VIT balance less unclaimed accruals, since sweeping the balance strands every unclaimed accrual behind an empty sovereign. That half TR6 had already got right, from the TR5 review's routing

## 08 §2.6's "both legs scale by the same factor" is…
authorized_by: Session, under R-1 (spec rulings delegated 2026-07-25; no D-n moves, no 02 surface moves). Raised by TR5 as a DONE_WITH_CONCERNS deviation rather than implemented silently
docs_touched: 08 §2.6 (the scaling bullet and the *Failure behaviour* paragraph); design §4.5 and §6

08 §2.6's "both legs scale by the same factor" is retired: the reward clamps to the budget's unpromised remainder and the debit is never reduced by budget pressure.

TR1 wrote that rule and TR5 found it is neither implementable nor safe. **The factor does not exist at settlement time** — settlement is pull-based and per-participant, so an epoch's total accrual is unknown until the last participant settles and there is no moment at which one `k` applies to both legs. **Every per-call approximation of `k` is orderable, and it is orderable by exactly the attacker the rule was written against:** a wash operator settles the winning account while the budget is full and takes `k = 1`, waits for other participants to exhaust it, then settles the losing account into a headroom of zero and takes `k = 0`. The pair nets `+r × scored` — the failure the scaling rule existed to prevent, reached *through* the rule. The legs also draw on **different pots** (reward on the authorized VIT budget, forfeit on `INSURANCE`), so no conservation identity ever linked them. The clamp keeps the bullet's second protection, because the rate stays fixed at `r` and only the total is capped, so no pair in a thin epoch takes the whole budget against a small forfeit. It gives up the first: under budget pressure a loser pays in full while a winner is clamped — the **R-7 direction**, since over-punishing cannot create an unbacked claim and under-punishing funds the farm. **The residual is stated rather than left to be found:** a thin budget is first-come-first-served, so a late settler may receive less than `r × scored`. A fairness cost, never a solvency one, and it cannot make any pair net positive. **⚠ TWO CLAUSES OF THIS ROW WERE RETRACTED ON 2026-08-11 and the row is kept only as a record — do not re-apply them.** *"No pair in a thin epoch takes the whole budget against a small forfeit"* and *"it cannot make any pair net positive"* are both the earning-cap claim, and the cap does **not** deliver that: it is proportional to each account's own bond, so an operator funding both legs sizes them asymmetrically and directs the profit. What actually bounds the pair is the **rate coupling** `99 × rwd.rate_ppb ≤ 200 × mkt.fee_ppb`, screened at the amendment boundary by TR9. The amendment this row records is itself unaffected — retiring symmetric scaling was right, and for the reasons given. Only its two supporting claims about the cap were wrong. This correction is inline because R-4 makes the Decision log what a resuming session reads, and this branch's own history is that a stale ruling gets re-applied. Found by the implementer, which built the ordering attack as a test and killed the symmetric-scaling mutant with it; the controller re-derived the attack independently before amending. The retired wording is corrected in **all four** places it reached — two in 08, two in the design — with the two code comments quoting it routed to the fix round

## The trading accuracy score would have confiscated the…
authorized_by: Session, under R-1 (spec rulings delegated 2026-07-25; no D-n moves, no 02 surface moves, so no contract bump). Raised by the TR4 implementer as the deepest of its four questions, and it was
docs_touched: 08 §2.6 (rules 1 and 4 plus two new paragraphs), 15 §4.1 (point 2); design §4.4; plan TR5 obligation 7

The trading accuracy score would have confiscated the bonds of accurate traders, and rule 4 now has three arms

(SQ-1051). Asked as a units question — the score adds plain USDC to branch-conditional USDC at par, and 15 §4.1 said "in USDC" without saying which — it was a defect. **A conditional market pays a buyer in two currencies and only one survives.** The trade wrapper splits `cost + fee` of plain USDC across both branches and sends `cost` of the traded branch to the book, so the buyer leaves holding the scalar position **and `cost` of mirror-branch branch-USDC**; a sale is paid in traded-branch branch-USDC, not in plain USDC. `received` and the mirror leg are worth par in exactly opposite states of the world, so the single rule `received − spent` could not be right in both — and it was wrong in the common one. [04](../../../../docs/architecture/04-markets-and-pricing.md) §6.2 guarantees as **G-3** that a buyer in the losing branch *"loses only fees when its branch is annulled"*. The old rule scored that buyer at `−(cost + fee)`: the whole notional against a realized loss of `fee`, about **1/333** of it at the `mkt.fee` default. Roughly half of all decision-market trading sits in the branch that does not realize, so the program would have debited the bonds of the traders it exists to pay — under fully green gates, because every conservation identity and every per-call assertion holds while it happens. **Ruled:** `MarketScore` gains `mirror_principal` (the sum of book-side `cost` over buys) and rule 4 splits — realized `received − spent`; **annulled `mirror_principal − spent`, discarding every `received` credit** because those are annulled-branch units worth nothing; **proposal VOID drops the entry at zero**, since §6.2 gives VOID its own delta and no forecast was tested. The annulled arm is **exact rather than conservative**: it reduces to `−Σfee`, which is G-3 restated. 15 §4.1 now requires all three arms generated and the annulled case asserted at exactly `−Σfee`, because `−(cost + fee)` is also negative and that is precisely the defect. **Third load-bearing find of this design, and the pattern is the same each time:** the claim was checked against its own arithmetic and never against the state of the world it does not cover

## The trading accuracy program excludes the hosted…
authorized_by: Session, under R-1 (values and spec rulings delegated 2026-07-25; no D-n moves, no 02 surface moves, so no contract bump)
docs_touched: 08 §2.6 (new *Only primary books are scored* paragraph + the anchor bullet), 13 §4 (`MaxScoredMarketsPerAccount` row), 14 (**TH-79**, separate from TH-78 because the two share no defense), 15 §4.1 point 3 (the closed-escape regression, stated as a **pair** because the exclusion is one boolean and an external-only test passes with scoring switched off entirely); design §9; plan TR5 obligation 5

The trading accuracy program excludes the hosted question service, and the reason is a bound doc 16 already published.

SQ-1049 arrived from the TR4 implementer as a sizing question: `MaxScoredMarketsPerAccount` is anchored to `MaxLiveMarkets` = 196, while `MaxLiveExternalMarkets` = 128 is enforced independently and `market.buy` serves `BookKind::External` through the same call path — so an account can hold positions in up to **324** live books and the anchor looked one term short. The answer is not a larger number. **16 §6.5 states that a client controlling a majority of its own named attestors can move the settled value, declines to repair it, and prices the residual: the blast radius is *that question's own escrow*, with *"Bleavit's ledger instance and every Bleavit market untouched."* ** Scoring an external book would break that published bound, converting an accepted, self-funded trust residual into a claim on the genesis `incentiv` pot — risk-free at up to `snapshot_bond / rate_headroom` per epoch. **Neither defense reaches it.** The bond does not, because a client who sets the outcome never loses. The rate coupling does not, because it bounds a wash *pair* and there is no offsetting loser here. **Two independent grounds agree.** The pot buys Bleavit's own decision liquidity — §2.6 justifies the whole program by 05 §5.2's `L̂` rising — and an external book raises no `L̂` while its client already pays `svc.fee_bps` for the venue. And rule 3 is unimplemented there regardless: it needs `settled_value`, and an external book settles through the separate service-ledger instance §2.6 never reads. Ruled: the accumulator skips `BookKind::External`, decided at the `buy`/`sell` call sites where `book.kind` is already in scope, so the exclusion costs **no** storage read and §2.6's "one extra storage read" stands. `MaxScoredMarketsPerAccount = MaxLiveMarkets` becomes exact rather than coincidentally safe, and 13 §4 now says why the second term is absent instead of leaving it to look like an omission. A trading incentive for the service, should it ever want one, must be funded by `svc.fee_bps`

## 07 §9 gains a normative *Commitments* block, because…
authorized_by: This session under R-1 (the user owns both sides of 02 §13's sign-off and delegated it; no 02 surface and no D-n is affected)
docs_touched: `docs/architecture/07-oracle-and-disputes.md` §4, §9

07 §9 gains a normative *Commitments* block, because the chain computes two `H256` values a third party must reproduce and neither had a rule.

`ack_observed` is admitted only against the round's current `report_hash` (§4), and `recompute_proof` authenticates a proof solely against the reporter's committed evidence hash (§9) — so a watchtower checking the stored commitment against the report it observed, and a prover producing a payload for a commitment, both need a derivation. The specification defined `report_hash` as opaque and evidence as merely "content-addressed", which left the rule readable only off the implementation. Both are now stated: BLAKE2b-256 over a domain-separated SCALE preimage, with disjoint domain constants, the payload length-prefixed and the report fields fixed-width. **No 02 change and no contract bump** — both values are still `H256` in the same §7.2 position, and a derivation is not a storage shape; the domain constants carry their own version suffix, so a later layout is a change to 07. Raised by the Codex connector on #299, which was right that an implementation-defined derivation of a value other parties must supply is exactly what R-1 forbids.

## Three implementation rulings from the whole-repository…
authorized_by: This session under R-1 (implementation rulings; the user requested the review and the fixes)
docs_touched: none — no `docs/architecture/` file changed

Three implementation rulings from the whole-repository security review. None amends the specification, and none moves an observable behavior of the shipped runtime.

(1) **The oracle's two commitments take an injected cryptographic hash.** 07 §9 requires "content-addressed raw data" and pins no function, so the choice was the implementation's — and what shipped was a 24-lane XOR fold with no preimage resistance, publishing its own preimage length in bytes 24..32. `hash_evidence` and `hash_report` now hash a domain-separated SCALE preimage with the runtime's blake2-256, injected by the pallet exactly as `question_service_core::verify_report_view_provenance` already takes one, so `crates/oracle-core/` stays FRAME-free. The stored shapes do not change — both are still `H256` — so 02 §7.2's frozen `RoundState` is untouched and no contract bump is owed. (2) **The 07 §9 evaluator becomes a `Config` seam, and this runtime binds `()`.** `oracle_core::recompute_value` is a model stand-in that reads the value out of the payload rather than deriving it under the frozen `formula_ref`; the A7 spec registry that would freeze one does not exist. A runtime cannot be allowed to settle from a stand-in, so the evaluator is now supplied by the runtime and the production binding refuses every payload. `recompute_proof` already returned `NotRecomputable` for every component, because `Recomputable` is empty and nothing writes it — this makes that structural instead of configurational, which is the whole point: one genesis entry could otherwise have armed a verifier that does not exist. Benchmarks bind a measuring evaluator under `runtime-benchmarks`, so the declared weight still bounds the settling path. (3) **The keeper refuses to sign against an endpoint it has not pinned.** Doc 01 §4.2 provisions RPC operators as a third-party role, and both pins defaulted to absent. The default is now refusal in live mode, with `--dry-run` and an explicit `--allow-unpinned-endpoint` as the two ways back — the second written down by the operator rather than assumed by the tool.
