## Doc 13 §5's proof-size shares were computed against the…

Doc 13 §5's proof-size shares were computed against the block-*length* ceiling, and are corrected.

§5 divided its pinned dispatched proof figures by **3,932,160 B** and published `decide` = 19.0 %, `settle_cohort(5)` = 18.5 % and `settle_cohort(12)` = 23.9 %. That divisor is a real limit of this runtime and the wrong one: it is 75 % of the 5 MiB `BlockLength`, so it bounds an extrinsic's **encoded length**, not the proof its execution generates. The proof budget is `pallet_welfare::primary_capacity()` — `MAXIMUM_BLOCK_WEIGHT`'s proof half is the relay's `MAX_POV_SIZE` = 10 MiB, less the quarter the N7 resource partition reserves for the external domain — so it is **7,864,320 B** and the shares are **9.5 %**, **9.3 %** and **12.0 %**. The two classes are not even scaled alike here: `BlockLength` scales Normal by `NORMAL_DISPATCH_RATIO` while `BlockWeights` deliberately exposes Normal to the whole physical block, so that `CheckWeight` cannot refuse an external call before the partition extension applies its quota. **Nothing was under-declared and no weight moved** — `pov_budgets.rs` always asserted against `normal_class_budget()` rather than against the prose, so the error lived only in the published share, in the direction of reporting the chain twice as full as it is. The same clause's `settle_cohort(5)` decomposition was stale in a second way and is re-derived from the generated file: `185,135 + 5 × 30,754 + 389,037` = **727,942 B**, matching the value E6 moved it to and the runtime already pins. **Found by recomputation, not by reading**: the explainer derives the trade ceilings from the generated `buy` weight instead of transcribing them, and 93 per block / 70 primary are unreachable from the smaller budget — it yields 46 and 35.

## A client precondition comparing a block against B′ must…
authorized_by: Implementation ruling (R-1; R-2 forbids the unanchored margin)
docs_touched: none (`docs/architecture/` untouched — the normative texts already say this). Code: `app/src/features/tx/src/guardian.ts` (hold horizon, approval expiry)

A client precondition comparing a block against B′ must treat B′ as spent (implementation ruling under R-1/R-2; no spec change).

11 §11.4 rule 2 fixes B′ as the **finalized** block the preconditions are read at, so nothing signed against it can be included in it and every dispatch runs at a strictly greater height. Bounds of the form `x >= now` therefore become **strict** in the client — a hold or an expiry whose last admissible block *is* B′ is already spent when the chain reads it, and offering it starts a proposal whose threshold approval is guaranteed to revert. Bounds of the form `x <= now + k` stay exactly as they are: the runtime's own `now` is higher at dispatch, so the client's `B′ + k` is the stricter of the two, and widening it would require a height the client does not have. **No inclusion margin is applied, and the refusal is deliberate.** Any margin beyond *\"B′ itself is spent\"* would be a claim about inclusion latency, or about how long a 7-member council takes to reach five signatures. Neither has an anchor in 11 §11.4, 11 §11.8.2, 02 §9, 13's parameters or a kernel constant, so under R-2 it would be a picked value. `B′ + 1` stays admissible, and a mutant adding `+ 1` is killed by the suite so the refusal is asserted rather than merely documented.

## 10 §2.1 claimed a brand cannot be produced by spread.…
authorized_by: R-1 (the user owns both sides of 02 §13's sign-off and has delegated it, 2026-07-25); doc 10 §2.1 is prose rather than an INV-FE text, and 02 is untouched, so `INTEGRATION_CONTRACT_VERSION` is unchanged

10 §2.1 claimed a brand cannot be produced by spread. It can, and the sentence was in the document the whole technique rests on.

Compiled, not argued: `{ ...genuine, field: substituted }` keeps a symbol-keyed brand in the inferred type and typechecks with **no assertion anywhere**, so `check:casts` — which looks for assertions — structurally cannot see it. A runtime probe then showed the forged value reaching a **permitting** control: a re-keyed closure permitted a bonded filing against `(41, 9)` while the read proved absence for `(40, 2)`, and a meter carrying `force_rerun`'s figures under the `delay_once` name reported allowance remaining. §2.1 now states that a brand proves *some producer ran* and never that the payload beside it is what that producer emitted, and that a control which **permits** on such a payload MUST carry a member a spread cannot reproduce. **`Finalized<T>` is deliberately exempted**, and that exemption is the interesting half: `derive(read, compute)` already grants exactly this power by design under §2.2's *\"computed client-side purely from such values\"*, so the spread is the sanctioned operation spelled differently rather than an escalation — and a spread still cannot forge a pin. Hardening it would break `derive`. Five types were fixed with a phantom non-exported `declare class` carrying one `#private` member, intersected with the existing shape: TypeScript drops private members from a spread type while keeping every ordinary property, so nominality costs **zero** consumer changes, zero fixture changes and zero runtime representation. Eight more are ranked and open as SQ-1038.

## 12 §1.5's expedited lane is unavailable, because it was…
authorized_by: Claude (R-1; raised by the Codex P1 on #286)
docs_touched: `12-release-and-operations.md` §1.5. Code: `app/tools/verify-release/verdict.ts`, `make-transcript.ts`, the release fixtures and suites

12 §1.5's expedited lane is unavailable, because it was never decidable from the published tree (SQ-1039 filed).

§1.5 states its admissible delta over source paths and has `diff-scope` check it over the built tree. Under §1.1 every chunk carries a content-hash-only name, and a chunk hash covers the filenames it imports, so a descriptor refresh renames its own chunk and then every chunk that imports it, up to the entry and `index.html`. The tool bridged that gap by allowlisting `assets/descriptors/` — a path that exists in its own fixtures and in no build. Ruled: remove the entry, refuse any candidate whose built-tree delta reaches app assets, and say why in the refusal. **The direction was the whole decision.** A lane that skips a 72 h soak may not open on a premise nothing evidences, while a soak taken unnecessarily costs time and nothing else, so the checker fails closed and §1.5 records that the policy stands while the evidence for it does not yet exist. What restores the lane is a build that gives descriptors a stable path, or a signed descriptor-scope declaration in `release.json`; SQ-1039 owns that choice rather than this ruling.

## 12 §1.4 gate 4 publishes the release-signature…
authorized_by: R-1 (the user owns both sides of 02 §13's sign-off and has delegated it, 2026-07-25); doc 12 is not 02, so `INTEGRATION_CONTRACT_VERSION` is unchanged
docs_touched: `docs/architecture/12-release-and-operations.md` §1.4 (gate 4 + one normative paragraph). Nothing outside doc 12 cites §1.4. Code: `app/tools/verify-release/cli.ts`, `compare.ts`

12 §1.4 gate 4 publishes the release-signature transaction ids, and §1.4 records why they cannot live in `release.json`.

Gate 4 listed the immutable TXID, attestation TXIDs and the gateway URL set, and never the **release-signature** TXIDs — although the release-signature floor added 2026-07-20 made ≥ 2 valid release-key signatures a verification requirement equal to attestations. So §1.4 required a count of credentials it gave no verifier any way to obtain. The Codex round on #286 surfaced the consequence: `compare` read `release_signatures` and `attestations` from the served document, `buildReleaseJson` emits neither, and every genuine release therefore verified with zero of each. **The obvious repair is wrong, and is now ruled out in the document itself.** §2.1 has release keys sign `release.json`'s SHA-256 hash and both verifiers check the *served bytes*, so a signature transaction exists only after those bytes are final, and writing its id back in invalidates the signature over it. Gate 4 therefore gains the release-signature ids, §1.4 gains a normative paragraph stating the circularity, and `verify-release compare` takes both sets as repeated `--signature`/`--attestation` arguments — reporting *no credentials named* distinctly from *none published*. The unattended §5.2 monitor cannot be handed flags and stays open as SQ-1035.

## 12 §1.3 publishes a command that runs, and §2.1 states…

12 §1.3 publishes a command that runs, and §2.1 states what an attestation signs.

Two amendments from the same Codex round. **(1) §1.3's published command could not execute.** It named neither `--transcript` nor `--gateways`, so the exclusive-option check rejected it before any request, and it omitted the keyring, both §1.2 manifest addresses and the credential transaction ids.
