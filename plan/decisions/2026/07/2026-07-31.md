## Oracle: a §5.3 default no longer settles forward, and…
authorized_by: User (owner for both sides of the 02 §13 sign-off under R-1; standing autonomous-resolution delegation)
docs_touched: 07 §3/§5.2/§5.3/§5.5/§6.2/§10; 02 §7.2 + §13 (v18); 00 (contract-version list); 14 TH-23/TH-24

Oracle: a §5.3 default no longer settles forward, and the §3 ladder survives exit (contract v19).

Two confirmed vulnerabilities, one PR. **(1)** `challenge` never checked distinctness and the close crank settled the challenger's counter-value with `flagged: false`, so one purse holding both roles moved a component by up to `Δs_max` while risking `0.6·B₁` — against 07 §6.3's own worked example, +102,000 instead of the intended −90,000, i.e. 8.6 % of the required ladder, repeatable every epoch with no post-settlement remedy (I-18). The repair is on the **value** side because it cannot be on the money side: 07 §5.3's own closing sentences forbid debiting a stack a party has not funded, so no rule can make a defaulting party forfeit the full ladder. A default now takes the §10 neutral path (carry-last, flagged) at **every** round, with the stack forfeit **100 % to INSURANCE at round 1** and 40/60 from round 2. `challenge` additionally refuses the round's own reporter — necessary but **not** sufficient (a second funded account defeats it), kept for state hygiene and as the anchor for a new try-state invariant. **(2)** `register_reporter` seated `offenses: 0` unconditionally and `deregister_reporter` deleted the row, so 07 §3's second-offense slash and third-offense ejection cost two extrinsics to erase. A new internal bounded `ReporterRecords` store retains the ladder across exit and ejection; re-registration carries the count and re-seats at the 07 §2(5) half stake past the second offense; ejection is permanent. `deregister_reporter` is now also blocked while the account is a live round's **challenger** (07 §3's "all rounds the reporter participated in" — a third, separately-found defect), which is what keeps a late verdict from landing on a departed account and lets the Codex-F17 unseated no-op stand unchanged. **Rulings (R-1):** a default advances **no** §3 offense — it is producible by the challenger, by a collator set censoring `counter_report` (14 TH-24) or by a dead node, so it is not a finding that the value was wrong, and recording one would hand a griefer a lever on a ladder that `orc.n_min` turns into a MetricSpec-admission veto; the round-1 bounty is denied because paying it makes griefing an honest *offline* reporter profitable, while §6.2's incentive survives from round 2 where consented escalation-then-abandonment evidences a real contest. **Rejected, recorded so they are not re-derived:** watchtower quorum on the default path (defeated at Δcost 0 by two self-registered seats — 07 §4's entity-independence rule has no implementation because 05 specifies no entity registry); flat bounty denial at all rounds (destroys the honest-challenger incentive); a second independent challenger (breaks the frozen single-valued `RoundState.challenger`); slashing `orc.reporter_stake` on default (a flat key against a value-scaled property — 5.7 % coverage at `StakeAtRisk` = 10M); a `stake == 0` tombstone seat (leaves the re-activation stake unheld — `apply_custody` has no increase arm). **Also corrected in passing:** `surface-manifest.json` pinned a stale four-variant `SettlePath` for the `ComponentValues` storage row while the event row pinned five (a release-time metadata binding would have failed on it; now pinned by a test in both places), and the second `INTEGRATION_CONTRACT_VERSION` literal in the runtime suite violated the v17 entry's own "exactly one literal" remedy and was removed. `transaction_version` untouched (02 §13 rule 7); pre-genesis, no migration; no new 13 key and zero limit-coverage churn.

## Proposal authorship and proposal funding are separated…
authorized_by: User (2026-07-31; owner for both the backend and frontend sides of the 02 §13 sign-off under R-1)
docs_touched: 02, 05, 06, 08, 14, 15, PLAN

Proposal authorship and proposal funding are separated (milestone E6).

`epoch.submit` required `proposal.proposer == who`, so the account that wrote the payload had to be the account that locked the 1k–50k USDC class bond — an intersection of two unrelated capabilities gating every one of the ~87 decisions/year the chain is designed to produce. The submit signer becomes the **funder** (the hold lands on the signer, so no account can be forced into a hold by someone else's extrinsic) and `proposal.proposer` becomes the **author**, free to differ. Four incidence rulings, all the user's. (1) `trs.proposer_reward` pays the **author** — it pays for authorial work, and `runtime/bleavit-runtime/src/configs.rs:8381` already pays `proposal.proposer`, so the ruling is preserved rather than built, and is pinned by a test precisely because a property that holds by accident is one that regresses silently. (2) Pre-qualification withdrawal is available to **either** party: neither is trapped, the author cannot strand the funder's capital indefinitely and the funder cannot hold a live proposal hostage. (3) The 06 §4 10 % non-decision-grade slash falls on the **funder**, necessarily, since the funder holds the bond — which is the reason the funder signs the exact payload rather than pre-authorizing an author in the abstract. (4) The ≤4-entries/epoch intake cap is re-keyed from the author to the **funder**. The fourth is not a preference but a defect closure: `crates/epoch-core/src/lib.rs:776` counts `p.proposer`, which is safe only while `submit` forces author == signer, and splitting the identities without re-keying would let one funder back 16 throwaway author addresses at 4 proposals each and take the whole 64-entry `IntakeQueue`, voiding 06 §4 rule 4 and the B-13 monopolization defense. Capital at risk is what prices spam, so the cap follows the capital. Author-only rights are unchanged: `bind_ratification` remains the proposer's, per 09 §1.1(4). Contract v17 → **v18** — `Proposal` and `ProposalSummaryView` each gain a trailing `funder: AccountId` under 02 §13 rule 3, no existing field moves, no call index moves so `transaction_version` is unchanged, and the chain is pre-genesis so no migration is required. No new 13 key: `intake.max_acct` keeps its value and bounds and only its subject changes.

## Splitting an identity creates a try-state obligation,…
authorized_by: Claude (implementation-level spec completion under R-1; no values-layer or contract-surface change, so no 02 §13 bump and no sign-off required)
docs_touched: 15, PLAN

Splitting an identity creates a try-state obligation, and 15 §4.1 now says so as a general rule (milestone E6).

Closing E6 exposed that the split had made a state representable which nothing checked: `ProposalBond.funder` names the account the hold was taken from, and before the split there was one identity, so the binding to `Proposal.funder` held by construction and no assertion had to state it. Post-split a bond keyed to the *author* is fully collateralized, sits inside its liability bound and is not orphaned — it passes every existing try-state assertion — while its refund and its 06 §4 slash both reach the wrong party. The obligation is written into 15 §4.1 in the general form ("wherever a custody record names one of two identities, try-state MUST bind that name to the identity the record carries") rather than as a `ProposalBond` special case, because the same hole opens on any future split and a rule stated once is cheaper than the same defect found twice. Implemented in `pallet-epoch`'s `do_try_state` with a test that passes on the honest state and fails once the bond is re-keyed.

## 05 §3.3's `k = 3` failure mode corrected: a permanent…
authorized_by: Claude (R-1; the claim was falsified by execution, the ceiling it justifies is not)
docs_touched: `05-welfare-and-decision-engine.md` §3.3

05 §3.3's `k = 3` failure mode corrected: a permanent 1-in-5 capacity forfeit, not a total halt.

The paragraph claimed that at `k = 3` "within a few epochs **every** `qualify` fails `TooManyCohorts` permanently, with no proposal able to enter measurement again". Executing the cohort machine falsified it: one cohort retires per epoch once the window fills, so admission succeeds four epochs in five indefinitely, with failures recurring at period `k + 2`. The error was reading a permanently saturated *live count* as permanent *admission failure* — a distinction the count cannot make, and the same conflation the first version of the S6 test made before the PR #200 Codex review caught it. **The kernel ceiling is unaffected**: a permanent, unrecoverable forfeit of one measurement epoch in every `k + 2` is decisive on its own, and it is unrecoverable because a cohort is per-epoch (turned-away proposals take T6 once, then T26). Corrected in place with the superseded sentence quoted, so no implementation looks for a halt that never arrives or reads its absence as evidence that `k = 3` is safe

## SQ-536 ruled: `collator.comp_epoch` re-anchored 2,000 →…
authorized_by: R-1/R-2 (values layer; user's standing autonomous-resolution delegation, 2026-07-25)
docs_touched: 00 (D-15), 13 §1, 08 §2.4/§10.1/§10.4/§10.5/§10.6, 12 §6.1

SQ-536 ruled: `collator.comp_epoch` re-anchored 2,000 → 500 against a published, governance-approved rate — reversing pass 1's decision to decline it.

Pass 1 declined this lever under R-2, on the grounds that its error direction is unsafe (underpaid collators stop authoring) **and** that no evidence anchored it. The first clause holds. The second was a statement about *this repository* mistaken for a statement about the world, and R-2's escalation test is a **conjunction** — so the deferral did not survive checking. R-1 warns that a deferred decision is often under-research in disguise; this was that. **The anchor:** Polkadot OpenGov referendum **#1870** (passed, executed) funds 38 system-parachain collators at **$250/collator/month**, **$307.24** fully loaded once the bounty's own hosting, curator and coordinator lines are spread across the funded set — **211.97 USDC/epoch** at a 21.0-day epoch. The superseded seed was **9.44×** that; the shipped one is **2.36×**. **Why the comparison is like-for-like rather than merely suggestive:** a Bleavit collator earns **nothing** from fees or tips — `OnChargeTransaction = FungibleAdapter<Balances, ()>` drops (burns) the imbalance, which is D-15's VIT-fee burn — so this line is its entire compensation, which is precisely why Polkadot's system parachains are treasury-funded rather than fee-funded. Verified against live sources and logged as **V-61**, including the one figure found and *rejected* as an anchor (a commercial $2,000–5,000/month estimate that bundles engineering headcount and happens to match the superseded seed — matching an unsourced estimate is not evidence). **Why the registry minimum specifically, and not a chosen intermediate:** R-2 says derive, never pick. The derivation is *take the lowest lawful value, then show it clears the anchor with margin* — reproducible from the registry plus one external source, with no room for preference. An intermediate like 1,000 would be a pick, since nothing in the evidence distinguishes it. **What makes seeding at the floor safe** — *as corrected the same day; this row's original text is superseded and the superseded reading is stated so the audit trail shows what was rejected*: the min is **unchanged** at 500 (the seed moved to the floor, not the floor to the seed), so all 20× of headroom is in the safe direction; recovery is ×2 per amendment on a 1-epoch cooldown; and `collator.comp_epoch` MUST be re-verified against operator quotes for **this** chain before production launch and before each enlargement of the collator set (13 §1, a release-checklist blocker). **Two justifications this row originally gave are withdrawn, both rejected on review and both of the same shape — leaning on a mechanism that protects something else.** (i) *"underfunding is fail-soft, so the failure mode is a delayed payment"* — false: `collator_compensation` computes the pool **from** the configured value and a successful payout clears the accumulator, so fail-soft catches an underfunded *line* while an underpriced *row* pays out in full and retains no unpaid difference. (ii) *"the launch set is invulnerable, so the market rate does not bind until Phase 4"* — false: invulnerability fixes an account's **selection** status and does not oblige anyone to keep authoring at a rate they will not accept; the launch operators are also the most likely to be standing up infrastructure for this chain, which is exactly the case the marginal-cost anchor does not cover. Neither is load-bearing anywhere in the shipped text. **Result: cost base 239,728 → 109,281/yr; runway to the *binding* 21.26M arming floor 15.6 → 34.3 yr at ZERO revenue; crossover `V*` 29.1M → 13.2M; quiet epoch 10,200 → 2,700.** Consistent break-even falls from **7** occupied slots (above `epoch.slots` = 5, hence unreachable) to **1**. Spec updated across 00 D-15, 13 §1 (+ a normative note deriving the anchor), 08 §2.4/§10.1/§10.4/§10.5/§10.6 and 12 §6.1; superseded rows struck rather than deleted.

## Ruled: renewal timing is a normative operating rule,…
authorized_by: Me, under R-1 (the user owns both sides of the 02 §13 sign-off and has delegated it, 2026-07-25); no 02 surface is touched, so no contract bump
docs_touched: `08-treasury-and-economics.md` §10.1, `09-execution-upgrades-and-rollout.md` §4, `deploy/runbooks/RB-TREASURY.md`

Ruled: renewal timing is a normative operating rule, and it is deliberately NOT enforced on-chain.

Two decisions, both mine under R-1. **(1) The spec retracts a claim it had been making.** 08 §10.1 said `ops.coretime` is "the one line that cannot be reduced", inferring un-reducible from unavoidable. The inference is false: `broker.renew`'s price is `min(leadin_factor(t)·end_price, max(prev·(1+bump), end_price))`, and `t` — how far into the leadin the renewal is submitted — is Bleavit's choice, worth 62× on cumulative 25-year spend and +18.3 years of runway (V-62, SQ-541). The paragraph now separates *cannot be removed* from *cannot be reduced* and keeps the true half: the line's level is still not derivable from the spec, because `end_price` is a market price. **(2) No on-chain price ceiling.** A quote-side ratchet or a max-price key was the obvious move and is refused, for three reasons that compound. It would need a fabricated number — a price ceiling has no kernel constant, no existing 13 §1 key and no published calibration behind it, and its unsafe direction is *refusing a renewal*, which loses a core. It is redundant against a control that already exists: `execute_coretime_renewal` debits `ops.coretime`, and refilling that line needs the `FutarchyTreasury` origin, so a sustained escalation *already* surfaces as a governance-visible refill request. And it would invert the correct risk ordering — the one failure this call may not risk is `SoldOut`, so any mechanism that can refuse a renewal on price grounds is more dangerous than the escalation it prevents. The rule is therefore stated in 09 §4 as an operating margin ("as late as the observed remaining-core margin allows, never later") with the procedure in RB-TREASURY, and the call stays timing-agnostic. **What this costs: the policy is unenforced and can be forgotten.** Accepted knowingly — the failure mode is a slow, fully reversible price ratchet whose signature is visible (rising refill cadence with flat `end_price`), and one late renewal repairs it entirely.

## SQ-540(b) ruled: the reserve haircut has THREE…
authorized_by: Me, under R-1; audit scope A, so the ruling is pinned by a regression test rather than left as prose
docs_touched: `08-treasury-and-economics.md` §1.2, `crates/futarchy-treasury-core/src/lib.rs` (test)

SQ-540(b) ruled: the reserve haircut has THREE maintenance carve-outs, and proposer rewards are not a fourth — they are not a carve-out at all.

The sweep reported 08 §1.2 enumerating two carve-outs against four code paths and framed it as a scope-A solvency divergence to be closed "either way". Both halves of that framing were wrong. **Collator compensation is a genuine third carve-out** and the argument is D-9's own, applied beneath itself: coretime keeps the chain's core, collator pay keeps someone willing to author on it, and without blocks the reserve probe never runs and the flag never clears — so gating it would make the freeze self-sealing. It is bounded to the dedicated `COLLATOR` pot, pays for work already performed, and `fund_budget_line` stays ungated under the flag because gating *that* would defeat carve-out (a). **Proposer rewards are a crystallized obligation**, in the class §1.2 already exempts as "existing stream claims". The check that decided it is the one that caught two P1s on the collator work: fail-soft protects against an unbacked claim, not against non-payment. `mark_executed` calls `do_proposer_reward` once and discards the result by design, so a haircut gate would **destroy** the reward rather than defer it — permanent forfeiture over a condition the proposer neither caused nor can influence, which G-1 and R-7 both forbid. Amending the code to "enforce the spec" would have been the unsafe direction here, and the spec was simply incomplete.

## SQ-540(e) implemented: the XCM execution fee is…
authorized_by: Me, under R-1; audit scope A, so both halves and the failure path are pinned by tests rather than left as prose. No 02 surface is touched
docs_touched: `08-treasury-and-economics.md` §10.2, `runtime/bleavit-xcm/src/trader.rs`, `runtime/bleavit-runtime/src/configs.rs`

SQ-540(e) implemented: the XCM execution fee is retained as treasury revenue, and DOT deliberately is not recognized.

Two decisions. **(1) Capture, not discard.** The chain already prices every inbound message at the governed 09 §6.1 rates; dropping the payment was unfinished wiring, not a policy. Worth stating precisely what dropping meant, because "burning revenue" overstates it: local USDC is backed by the sovereign reserve, so a burned fee left the reserve over-backing issuance — real protocol value, but off-ledger, invisible to `nav()`, and reachable only by a governance reconciliation. Routing it to `MAIN` leaves issuance and the reserve in the same relation and makes the value spendable. **(2) USDC is recognized as NAV; DOT is deposited but not.** 08 §1.2 marks DOT at 0 in NAV, so crediting DOT to `MAIN` would inflate NAV by exactly the term the treasury refuses to count — custody is still taken, since the asset is real, but recognition is USDC-only. The ordering is also load-bearing and is asserted: recognition follows a successful deposit and never precedes it, so this path cannot make the internal ledger claim more than the pot holds (I-33), while a deposit *without* recognition would be equally wrong in the other direction — NAV reads the internal ledger, so the revenue would exist and be unusable.
