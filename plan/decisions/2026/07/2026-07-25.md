## SQ-495 — 07 §6.3's coverage rule binds after admission,…
authorized_by: Chralt98 (delegated per 2026-07-25 standing instruction)
docs_touched: 07 §6.3; 13 §1 (`orc.bond_bps`, `orc.rounds`)

SQ-495 — 07 §6.3's coverage rule binds after admission, screened at the amendment boundary.

The rule was evaluated once, at `register_spec`, and both its inputs are lawfully amendable: `orc.rounds` carries no max-Δ at all and `orc.bond_bps` 250 → 150 sits inside its `Factor(2)` band, so a component admitted at 1,750 bps of coverage could keep settling money at 450. Ruled for the **governance-boundary** screen over the snapshot-time re-check — refusing the amendment while every admitted component is still fully collateralized, rather than admitting it and then owing a disposition for a component that has become under-covered mid-life, which would have to either neutralize a component whose data is fine or let it settle uncovered. Scope is every **registered** version, not just the frozen ones, because a version registered today activates later against today's ladder. Directional: raising coverage is always legal, and the screen stands aside entirely while nothing is admitted. 07 §6.3's recorded gap becomes a normative obligation; both 13 §1 rows record the screen.

authorized_by: Delegated (R-1, 2026-07-25)

SQ-492 — 07 §11(1)'s retention bound is normative, and expiry refunds both stacks rather than forfeiting either.

§11(1) already said retention \"is bounded by the track's own schedule (7 d decision + 1 d confirm), after which the stack resolves and the entry is reaped\", and nothing implemented the *after* — so for the one round the close crank cannot resolve (round `R_max` carrying a live challenge, where neither party is in default because both funded the round) \"until a terminal verdict\" meant forever. §11(1) now states the deadline (`retain_until = neutralized_at + decision + confirm`) as an implementation obligation, and states the disposition, because leaving it to be inferred is what would have gone wrong: §11(4) *reads* like a forfeiture rule and is not one. It prices the capital lock-up an attacker pays for buying only status quo — which is why 14 carries it as a threat-model row rather than a slash — so at expiry there is no adjudicated wrong side and taking custody of either stack would be a claim with no finding behind it (R-7). A verdict landing inside the window still forfeits per §5.5.

## SQ-141 — the registry lifecycle is versioned by…
authorized_by: Chralt98 (delegated per 2026-07-25 standing instruction)
docs_touched: 07 §7; 02 §6/§13

SQ-141 — the registry lifecycle is versioned by `(epoch, spec_version)`, and the reader's absence rule is two-way.

The ruling was made in batch X; the implementation settled three things the ruling could not. **Scope:** only `Aggregates` is re-keyed — `Filings`/`FilingCount` stay epoch-keyed so filing ids stay unique and none of the ten 02 §6 filing events is touched. **Safety of dropping the shared filing count at the first close:** `file` requires `now ≤ filing_window_end`, `close_epoch` requires `now > filing_window_end`, so no filing can arrive once any version has closed; the windows are disjoint by construction, not by convention. **The reader:** an unconditional refusal on a missing aggregate would have wedged the chain, because `close_epoch` deliberately refuses a never-filed epoch (anti-griefing) and most epochs therefore never carry a record. 07 §7 now distinguishes *no registry footprint* (the neutral 1.0 is honest — the absence is the evidence) from *a footprint lacking this version* (refuse). My first draft of that rule was the absolute form and was wrong. **Plus a reap-ordering rule** (`ReapNotDue`): a version may not be reaped while a sibling still holds unclosed records, or the epoch's only remaining evidence of having been filed is erased — stranding the sibling's close *and* flipping the reader to the favourable branch.

## SQ-496 — `epoch.horizon_k`'s ceiling is derived from…
authorized_by: Chralt98 (delegated per 2026-07-25 standing instruction)
docs_touched: 05 §3.3, 13 §1

SQ-496 — `epoch.horizon_k`'s ceiling is derived from I-21, not chosen, and is kernel-bounded.

13 §1 published a hard max of 4 while 05 §3.3's own cohort decomposition (`k measuring + 1 awaiting oracle + 1 settling ≤ 4`) forces `k ≤ 2`; `EpochParams::validate` mirrored the wrong bound. At `k = 3` steady state needs five concurrent cohorts, so every `qualify` fails `TooManyCohorts` permanently — a chain-wide wedge reachable by a lawful META+values amendment inside the key's published range. Ruled: ceiling = `MAX_NON_TERMINAL_COHORTS − 2`, kernel-bounded so `amend_registry` cannot restore the wedge. The same ceiling is what makes the `≤ 2 concurrent frozen versions` factor true in `MAX_ROUNDS = 16 × 4 × 2` and in 07 §7's per-version aggregates, so 05 §3.3 now states that coupling normatively rather than leaving four documents to assume it. Surfaced while deriving SQ-141's storage bound.

SQ-491 — 07 §4's "an epoch with ≥ 1 open round" is oracle-owned and latched, and only the most recently ended epoch is swept.

§4 stated the predicate and assigned it no owner, no storage and no derivation. ## Two adjacent questions ruled without opening rows.
authorized_by: Chralt98 (delegated)
docs_touched: — (rulings only)

(i) *Does a 05 §4.8 dead-man pause shift the money deadline?* Yes, and that is correct: the pause halts the epoch clock, so the schedule-derived deadline waits while absolute-block `challenge_deadline`s keep running. ## SQ-296 — the registry filing bond is value-scaled at…
authorized_by: Me, under the R-1 decide-and-proceed delegation (2026-07-25)
docs_touched: 07 §7 (new normative filing-bond note; `file`/`challenge_filing` cells; fixed-window note; errors + audit concerns; §12 merit floor; §13 audit concerns; §7 parameter summary), 13 §1 (`reg.bond_*` role, `orc.bond_bps` second consumer), 15 I-28 (stored-bond custody wording)

SQ-296 — the registry filing bond is value-scaled at creation, on §6's existing rate; no contract bump.

07 §7 already required an escalating registry dispute to carry "§6 value-scaled bonds", and §6.1's rate is `orc.bond_bps` (live, 250 bps, kernel-bounded, 150 bps hard min). The ruling moved *when* the scaling applies — filing time instead of escalation time — not at what rate, so **no new parameter was needed** and R-2 step 1 resolves by reuse. `F(kind, m) = max(reg.bond_{kind}, ceil(orc.bond_bps × Exposure(kind, m) / 10,000))`, rounding up and `max`-after-rounding exactly as §6.1's *Units and rounding* rule requires (I-4/I-28 custody direction). The `reg.bond_*` keys keep their values and change **role**, from the bond to its floor; deliberately not `orc.bond_floor`, which would double the incident bond at zero exposure and leave both 13 keys consumerless. **The Incident exposure set is every cohort consuming the measurement epoch, not the consumers of one component** — `I` is not a `MetricId` at all but a scalar multiplier welfare applies unconditionally to `C_attested` and `C_settlement`, so pricing it against one component's consumers would under-collateralize it by construction. The Milestone set is component-scoped and **not determinable** until SQ-175 binds that aggregate, so `file` refuses (`ExposureUnavailable`, G-1) on that instance — the disabled posture the ruling mandates, now narrowed to the instance that genuinely lacks its input. Escalation-time re-bonding **retired**: an unchallenged false S1 filing never escalates, is refunded on `Upheld` and still drives `I` to 0, so creation-time pricing is what closes the cheapest attack. Frozen at creation and read from the record thereafter, including the challenger's matching bond — a live recompute would let a lawful mid-window META amendment price a challenger out of a filing already custodied at the old amount. **No `INTEGRATION_CONTRACT_VERSION` bump:** 02 §7 freezes the registry's storage and events, not its errors, so the new variant rides along as a **trailing** addition (trailing is required — shifting error indices is metadata-visible). Two superseded rationales corrected rather than quietly dropped: 07's fixed-window note rested on filing bonds being *flat*, and §12's merit floor on their being *"flat, smaller"*; both premises are now false, so the window argument is re-based on what filings decide and the merit-floor exclusion on the structural fact that `ProcessHold` iterates oracle rounds only — which is what keeps it durable now that a filing bond can reach or exceed `B_1`.

## SQ-294 ruling — the registry's counter-round is…
authorized_by: Autonomous resolution under the standing R-1 delegation (2026-07-25)
docs_touched: 07 §7 (Calls row + terminal-resolution note, errors, audit concerns)

SQ-294 ruling — the registry's counter-round is terminal, and its discretion is bound rather than removed.

07 §7 offered two closure paths and neither exists: `recompute_proof` needs a `formula_ref` a bonded off-chain-fact claim does not have (§9), and escalation into §5 step 4 needs a `(component, epoch)` game that an Incident filing has no component for — `I` is a scalar multiplier, not a `MetricId` (05 §4.4). So the `OracleResolution` verdict the code always used is the real path, and §7 now says so. Because no on-chain quantity decides it, three constraints are normative: the committed evidence hash must be restated (a commitment binding, not a proof — `MaxEvidenceLen = 32` leaves no preimage to verify, and its job is to stop a mis-parameterised verdict slashing an unrelated bond); the counter-round window must have elapsed (it was stored state with no reader); and the forfeit is the stored bond (I-28). Removing the authority was rejected: it is the only exit from `Challenged`, and an unresolved challenge resolves *favourably* through welfare's pull-side default, not neutrally.

SQ-295 ruling — §5.4's "only" scopes to the game, not the track.

The `OracleResolution` track carries two deliberately different disputes: `oracle.adjudicate` and §7's `registry.resolve_challenge`. ## SQ-461 ruling — 09 §1.2(7) is a rate-meter check and…
authorized_by: Autonomous resolution under the standing R-1 delegation (2026-07-25)
docs_touched: 09 §1.2 (normative note); 11 §precondition-mirror row 8

SQ-461 ruling — 09 §1.2(7) is a rate-meter check and nothing wider.

Item 7's one line admits two wrong readings, and the code held one of each. It must *actually run* for all three meter families before dispatch: delegating treasury/issuance to "the dispatched calls themselves" is not equivalent, because a mid-batch meter refusal is recorded as a T18 execution failure and bounds the retries, while item 7 grants a contended proposal the **full grace window**. And it must *refuse nothing else*: "meters admit the batch" is not "the batch would succeed", so a payload failing a dry-run for a non-meter reason (insufficient funds, NAV floor, stream threshold, bad line) belongs to item 12, where the recorded failure and bounded retry are correct for a defect that waiting cannot fix. `code.spacing` is the one meter the guard must evaluate itself, because it alone carries the D-9 expedited exemption. `meters_clear` (02 §4) answers item 7 and only item 7, and is fail-closed on an unreadable payload. Recorded in 09 §1.2 as a normative note; 11's mirror row points the frontend at the field. No 02 surface change — the field's documented meaning was already this, and the implementation was simply non-conformant, so no `INTEGRATION_CONTRACT_VERSION` bump.
