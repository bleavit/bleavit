## Item

**10 §8.4's import-quota sentence is four controls, not one, and three of them are about *when***

## Spec ref

10 §8.4, §9.2

## Status

**verified 2026-08-05 by mutation over each clause**

## Result

*"≤ 400 MB uncompressed, ≤ 4 M rows, **streamed**, **eviction preview before import**"*. **"Streamed" is where the quota is enforced**, not a performance note: a check that runs after the file is read is enforced by the browser running out of memory, which is a crash with a moral rather than a refusal — so `admitChunk` refuses at the chunk that crosses the line and there is no variant reporting a breach afterwards. **Both bounds, because either alone is trivially evaded**: 400 MB in 100 rows and 4 M rows totalling 10 MB each sit inside one bound and outside the other, and both break something real (memory, and an IndexedDB row count that presents as a tab which never becomes responsive). **"Before import" is the whole of the third**: §9.2's ladder means a large snapshot evicts existing data, and the user's decision is whether to trade what they have for what the snapshot offers — which they can only make *while they still have both*. `planImport` therefore writes nothing and the entry point requires its result, the same device `admitIntent` uses for its digest. Eviction is **oldest-first** (§9.2 gives up depth before recency; evicting the newest data to store a snapshot of old data is the exact inversion of the user's intent) and **stops as soon as it has enough**, and an infeasible import is **reported rather than thrown** — a plan that refuses to be constructed cannot show why it does not fit, and *"import failed"* with no reason is what makes somebody delete their local data by hand and retry
