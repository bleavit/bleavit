## Item

**FE-P11's load-bearing half: CSP `connect-src` cannot govern the §13.4 handoff transports**

## Spec ref

10 §12 (FE-P11); 10 §13.4; 12 §5.1; INV-FE-6

## Status

**resolved 2026-08-04** against the primary specifications

## Result

10 §12 marks this half "load-bearing for §13's INV-FE-6 claim, so it is verified rather than assumed", and says a negative result "blocks §13 entirely and is the outcome to look for first". **The result is positive, and the mechanism is the reason rather than the API list.** CSP fetch directives are enforced through exactly one hook: *"Should request be blocked by Content Security Policy?"*, called at step 2.4 of **Main Fetch** (plus the response check at step 11) — so a capability that never enters Fetch cannot be reached by `connect-src` whatever it does. Checked one by one: **Web Share** transmits to the share target "through the operating system's sharing mechanism, not via HTTP requests", mentions CSP nowhere, and is governed by Permissions Policy `web-share` (default allowlist `'self'`); the **async Clipboard API** "operate[s] entirely on the system clipboard without initiating HTTP communications", mentions CSP nowhere, and is governed by the Permissions API plus transient activation; **File System Access** likewise, governed by transient activation and its own permissions model. **The consequence that matters for the build:** 12 §5.1's claim — "none of which is a fetch, so none is governed by `connect-src`" — is correct, *and it cuts the other way too*. Because CSP structurally cannot see these transports, `check:handoff-network` is not a redundant second gate: it is the **only** mechanical control over egress on a handoff path, which is why V-86/V-92 hardening it mattered more than it looked. **Still open:** FE-P11's *first* half — `navigator.share({ files })` availability across the browser/OS matrix — needs a device lab, so 10 §12's conservative assumption stands unchanged: **clipboard and file are primary, Share is a fallback**
