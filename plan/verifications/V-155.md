## Item

**A test fixture derived the block hash from the chain id, which made a chain check and a block check indistinguishable**

## Spec ref

11 §11.9.1; 15 §4.8

## Status

**fixed 2026-08-05**

## Result

`fundingReaders` refuses two readers on the same chain — the `readDepositInputs(reader, reader, …)` slip, after which every Asset Hub figure is a futarchy-chain read under an Asset Hub label. The suite looked thorough and was not: the reader fixture computed `blockHash` from `chain`, so **every same-chain pair also shared a block hash and every cross-chain pair also differed**. A mutant comparing `blockHash` instead of `chain` therefore passed the entire suite. Caught by the mutation sweep rather than by review, and it is the precise inverse of the discipline applied one commit earlier: `provenance.test.ts` and `derive.test.ts` deliberately hold the **block hash equal across two chains** so only the chain check can refuse, and here the fixture had welded the two together instead. Repaired by making `blockHash` an explicit argument and building both directions — the same-chain pair at *different* blocks (a block check would not refuse it) and the cross-chain pair at the *same* block and height (a block check would refuse it). Re-run: the block-comparing mutant now fails both, plus seven others. **The general lesson is about helpers rather than about this check**: a fixture factory that derives one field from another silently removes the ability to vary them independently, and nothing in the suite reads as wrong afterwards — every test still passes, still looks specific, and no longer distinguishes the two properties it names.
