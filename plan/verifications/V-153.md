## Item

**My own resume note said the Asset Hub chain joins at boot; 11 E17 says it must not**

## Spec ref

11 E17; 10 §9.3; 11 §11.9.1

## Status

**corrected 2026-08-05**

## Result

The F18 note read *"`startTopology` gains a third chain sharing the relay via `potentialRelayChains`"*. `startTopology` **is** the boot path, and 11 E17's L column says *"AH connection syncs on entering the flow (lazy — the AH chain is not connected at boot)"* — with 10 §9.3 budgeting the chain specs as *"relay + para + Asset Hub, gz, **lazy**"*, so the laziness is stated twice, in two documents, in the columns that own it. Implemented as written, every session would have paid a third chain's memory and sync whether or not it ever deposited, and an Asset Hub sync failure would have landed **inside the boot machine**, where the only thing it could do is degrade a state that has nothing to do with it. Caught by reading E17 before writing the code, which is the only reason it was caught at all — the note was mine, it was recent, and nothing mechanical disagrees with it. **This is the third instance in three days** (V-147, V-151), and it sharpens their shared repair rather than repeating it: those two were *stale* notes contradicted by a machine-checked artifact, and this one was never right — written from the shape of the existing code (`startTopology` takes `relay` and `para`, so a third chain looks like a third field) instead of from the spec section that governs it. A resume note is a **plan**, and R-2 applies to plans exactly as to code: read the owning section first, and cite it in the note so the next reader can check the claim instead of inheriting it.
