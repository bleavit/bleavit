## Item

**`SyncDegraded` is reachable — the ruling, the driver, and the refusal it is built around**

## Spec ref

10 §3.1 (amended); 10 §12 (FE-P2)

## Status

**ruled and implemented 2026-08-05**

## Result

SQ-597 asked which of three options should feed 10 §3.1's peer-count transitions. **Ruled (a)+(c): depend on `system_health`, name the dependency in the spec, and specify what happens when it cannot be read.** Option (b) — re-specifying the transition on a spec-compliant observable alone — was refused on two grounds that only appear once you write it out: stalled `chainHead` follow progress cannot distinguish *no peers* from *a chain that stopped*, and before `initialized` there are no follow events **by definition**, so a naive no-progress timer fires on every cold start slower than the window — precisely the warp sync it would be watching. It also leaves §3.1's own diagnostics panel (*per-bootnode dial results, port-443 note, add-bootnode*) describing data the client would no longer obtain. What makes (a) acceptable is that the dependency is on **smoldot**, which INV-FE-1 already makes the entire trust root — depending on one of its JSON-RPC methods adds no trust — and the instability is a **bump-time** risk behind an exact pin, so the mitigation is a gate rather than a design: `check:smoldot-surface` fails the build if a bump removes a method the pinned PAPI issues. **The driver** is `packages/chain-client/src/health.ts`, pure and timer-free like `boot.ts` and for the same reason — the conditions it exists for only occur when something is broken, so they must be testable with nothing running. Its whole shape is the one refusal SQ-597 turns on: **absence is not zero.** `readingFromHealth` has **four** arms, and every malformed reply lands on `indeterminate` rather than `no-peers` — a missing, non-numeric, negative or fractional count says nothing about connectivity, and the one reading that must never be manufactured is the one that degrades. Zero peers with `shouldHavePeers` **absent** is `indeterminate` too, because assuming `true` would turn a legitimately peerless chain into a permanent degradation; zero with `shouldHavePeers: false` gets its **own** arm, since a successful read of a non-fault is a different thing from a read that failed. Non-degrading readings **freeze** the clock rather than clearing it — a reader that breaks mid-outage must not resolve the outage by going quiet — and a **change of cause restarts** it, so one fault is never reported as having held for the duration of another. The threshold is a **required argument** (relay 60 s, para 30 s), a backwards or non-finite clock is refused rather than acted on, and the fallback path degrades under an **`indeterminate` diagnosis** rather than claiming *no peers*. **5 mutations, 0 survived** — including *indeterminate degrades unconditionally* and *a missing peer count reads as zero peers*, the two that would have shipped the exact failure the rule forbids. 70 chain-client tests green; all 13 app gates pass. The doc-10 derived copy in `docs/design/claude-design-kit/` was regenerated with it and asserted byte-identical below its header, since AGENTS.md forbids leaving that pack stale.
