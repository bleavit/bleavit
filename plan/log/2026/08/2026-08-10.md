## **TR5 ✅ — folding, epoch settlement and the timeout…

**TR5 ✅ — folding, epoch settlement and the timeout escape; a normative rule retired**

**Five of nine tasks land, and this one refused to implement a sentence of the specification — correctly.** TR5 built the settlement half: `settle_market_score` folds one settled market and deletes the entry, `settle_epoch` closes a participant's epoch once, and an absolute block-height timeout stops a never-settling market from locking a bond. It also carried **seven** routed obligations and moved the stored `MarketScore` shape once for both new fields. **08 §2.6 required that an over-subscribed budget scale the reward and the debit by one factor. That rule is neither implementable nor safe, and it fails against exactly the attacker it was written for.** Settlement is pull-based and per-participant, so an epoch's total accrual is unknown until the last participant settles and no single factor exists at settlement time. Every per-call approximation is *orderable*: settle the winner while the budget is full for `k = 1`, wait for others to exhaust it, settle the loser into a headroom of zero for `k = 0`. The reviewer built it and printed the numbers — budget 300, reward 250, forfeit **0**. The reward now clamps to the unpromised remainder at the fixed rate and the debit is never reduced by budget pressure; the reviewer proved that safe across all 60 budget × ordering combinations and then analytically, since the clamp only lowers a reward and the debit is order-independent. **Strictly safer than the rule it replaced, not merely equivalent.** **The review's sharpest finding was against my amendment, not the code.** Its justification re-asserted the retired earning-cap claim — *"no pair in a thin epoch can take the whole budget against a fixed small forfeit"* — which the reviewer falsified with **no budget pressure at all**: winner bonded 1,000,000 takes 250 against a loser bonded 1,000 forfeiting 100. That is TR2's Critical verbatim, and I added a **fifth** instance of it while correcting the four TR9 already owns. Third wrong location list in this plan, second one mine. **Two disclosures the review earned:** an unset or zero `rwd.rate` discharges every outstanding debit — the G-1 behaviour is right, but 13 §1's "safe direction" covers new scoring and not scores already folded — and the first-come-first-served budget residual is *choosable*, since whoever gains most from settling first is whoever already knows their score. **Mutation coverage:** the reviewer ran 32 and killed 31, agreeing with the report on the one survivor, and one mutation was refused by the compiler because lowering the score-entry lifetime below the settlement horizon trips a const assertion. ## **TR6 ✅ — the treasury funding call, and a normative…

**TR6 ✅ — the treasury funding call, and a normative rule that could not be implemented**

**Six of nine tasks land.** `fund_trading_rewards` is one bounded `FutarchyParam` leaf that returns the previous epoch's unspent budget and authorizes the next, in that order, so the two never race. **The rule retired here was unimplementable, not merely awkward.** 08 §2.6 said both settlement legs scale by the same factor; that factor does not exist at settlement time, because settlement is pull-based and the epoch's demand is unknown until it closes. Every approximation is orderable by exactly the attacker the rule was written against. The reward now clamps to the budget's unpromised remainder at a fixed rate and **the debit is never reduced** — non-positive for a wash pair at every budget level and in every settlement order. **A second defect was a one-extrinsic denial of the whole program:** the budget return was permissionless while the accumulator resets unconditionally, so anyone could empty the headroom mid-settlement for a transaction fee and every later settler would close at zero with their score discarded. The return now carries the authorization's own origin.
