## Question

**§8.3 says a probe happens *"on enable"* and also that *"only `Disabled` stops reads"*, and a source between enabling and its first answer must be one or the other.** The section opens *"Per-provider health probe on enable + every 10 min; `Healthy → Slow → Failing → Disabled(auto, reason)`"* and closes by making the ladder's **shape** normative, ending *"only `Disabled` stops reads, always with a reason"*. Those two sentences do not agree about the gap between the user's click and the first response: reading the second literally, the source serves immediately and the probe-on-enable requirement decides nothing, which is what the client did until an `unprobed` state was added; reading the first literally, there is a state before the ladder that the degradation sentence never mentions. F9 takes the second reading as a statement about the **four ladder states** and refuses `unprobed` under INV-FE-12's fail-closed lattice — that is a choice, not a derivation. **Three things a ruling must settle**: does the ladder gain a fifth state in §8.3's own text; does `Failing` serve (F9 now says yes on the strength of *"only `Disabled`"*, having wrongly excluded it while nothing consulted the predicate); and **what a probe consists of** — §8.1 gives a suggestion an `endpoint` and §8.3 says probe it, and no document names the request, the success condition, or what a *snapshot* provider answers, which is what leaves F24's driver unwritable under R-2

## Status

**resolved 2026-08-07 (F24, 10 §8.3 + §8.5.3)** — the two sentences do not actually disagree, and F9's reading was right. *"Only `Disabled` stops reads"* is normative about **the four ladder states**: it forbids `Slow` and `Failing` from stopping reads and says nothing about a source that has not entered the ladder. `unprobed` is that state, it is not on the ladder, and it does not serve — the ladder being the only control the client has over a third-party endpoint, a source serving before its first probe has escaped it entirely for as long as that probe takes. §8.3 now says so. What made the reading *cheap* rather than merely correct is the other half of F24: §8.5.3's driver fires the probe **on enable**, so `unprobed` lasts one round trip instead of forever, and a snapshot import was never gated on the ladder anyway (SQ-773) **Extended 2026-08-07 by the R-6 re-review, and the extension matters more than the original ruling.** SQ-771 asked about `unprobed`, and the answer — a state before the ladder that serves nothing — was right but incomplete, because the same non-monotonicity lived one arm over. §8.3 licenses `Failing` to serve on the stated ground of *"one timeout in a healthy series"*, and a source whose **first** probe timed out has no series: it went `unprobed` (serving nothing) → `failing` (serving), so failing to answer bought the eligibility that not being asked withheld. §8.3 now states that `Failing` serves only after the source has answered at least one probe, and `ProviderHealth.failing` carries `everAnswered` so the clause is checkable rather than assumed. The counter runs either way, so a source that never answers still reaches `Disabled` on schedule.
