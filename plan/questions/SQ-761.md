## Question

**10 §6.3 says a range with no edge is *"refused"* and gives a rationale that argues for keeping it, so the sentence decides its own case both ways.** The text: *"A range whose edge is missing is refused rather than checked: every comparison against an absent field is false, so a missing edge would report as *corrupt* rather than as *unverifiable* and the client would drop honest ranges on a schema slip."* The rationale is that dropping honest ranges is the harm — which argues for **keeping** an edgeless range as unverifiable — while *"refused"* reads as **dropping** it, and the paragraph's own closing rule (*"a check that cannot be performed keeps the range"*) points the other way again. F8 drops: `assertWellFormed` throws on a missing edge and `sanitizeCoverage` disposes of the range. That is defensible — an edgeless range can never be checked, so keeping it forever is keeping an unverifiable claim — but it is the reading the rationale argues against, and the cost is real: one schema slip empties an index the section wanted preserved. **Ruling needed:** does *"refused"* mean dropped, or kept-and-never-checked?

## Status

**open — raised by the R-6 re-review of F8.** The shipped reading is the stricter one and loses data on a schema slip; the alternative retains a claim nothing can verify. Filed to **B7**
