## Question

**A block-denominated enactment delay cannot prove epoch boundaries were crossed, because the logical epoch clock is crank-driven.** SQ-234 sized the `entrenched` delay at four maximum-length epochs of blocks, which is four boundary *crossings* exactly while the clock is advancing — and `epoch.tick` is permissionless `ensure_signed`, never a timer. `pallet-referenda` schedules enactment at `now + min_enactment_period` regardless, so on a chain nobody ticks for the whole delay an entrenched decision can enact having opened **no** new Baseline market, i.e. with none of the priced second opinion 06 §2.4 exists to buy. Closing it needs an epoch index captured at approval and re-checked at dispatch; referenda exposes no on-confirm hook, so it takes a two-phase **arm/apply** surface: the referendum enacts an `arm` call that records `(target, armed_at_epoch)`, and a second permissionless `apply` refuses until `current_epoch >= armed_at + 4`. That adds storage, an authority-matrix row per armable call, and a dispatch that can fail and need re-attempt — a liveness surface — which is why SQ-234 shipped the sizing instead.

## Status

**✅ resolved 2026-07-26 (batch X)** — **ruled: the arm/apply surface is refused**, and 06 §2.4 now carries the decision rather than a tracked gap. No code change. Three reasons, in order of weight. (i) *The delay cannot buy what a stalled chain cannot sell.* The four boundaries exist to open new Baseline markets; on a chain whose clock is not advancing those markets do not open, so the second opinion is unavailable under either design — arm/apply does not deliver it, it withholds the decision until someone restarts the clock, and once anyone does, `tick` is permissionless and the boundaries accrue anyway. The designs differ only on a chain that stays dead: enact-without-opinion versus never-enact. (ii) It pays for that with a permanent new liveness dependency on the one track designed to be hardest to move — an `apply` that must actually be called, on the critical path of *every* entrenched decision, trading a clock assumption reachable only on a halted chain for a keeper assumption on all of them. (iii) **The quantitative point the row missed:** the delay is four *maximum-length* epochs, so it yields exactly four crossings only at the 42 d ceiling — at the 21 d default it yields **eight**. Losing the fourth boundary needs a clock that has lost more than half its expected advancement across 168 days, which is the halt 05 §4.8's detector already answers, not a subtle shortfall
