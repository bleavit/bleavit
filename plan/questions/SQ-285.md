## Question

Should the dead-man **recovery epoch expose an exit for intake bonds**? Shipped behaviour closes `withdraw` for the whole recovery epoch: the legal guard is `proposal.epoch == CurrentEpoch`, recovery is a fresh index (resume increments the clock) and `submit` is refused throughout, so no proposal can satisfy it — a proposer whose bond is held cannot exit for ~1 epoch. Bounded deferral, not forfeiture: the bond stays held and withdrawal reopens when carry-forward re-anchors the proposal on the roll ending recovery. But G-1 argues an exit that only *releases* funds and advances nothing should not be gated on a liveness incident the proposer did not cause. Options: (a) ratify the deferral as-is; (b) relax `withdraw`'s epoch guard to accept any `Submitted` proposal while recovery is set, cancelling and refunding immediately; (c) re-anchor carried-forward proposals at the *start* of recovery rather than at the roll that ends it, which restores the guard without touching `withdraw`. (b) and (c) are code; (c) interacts with the intake-queue admission bound.

## Status

**resolved 2026-07-20 (ratified as shipped; delegated ruling).** Option (a): the deferral stands. 05 §4.8 now states it normatively. The reasoning that closed it is that the G-1 test was misapplied when this row was raised — G-1 constrains what the protocol *does* on a failure path, and declining to process an exit is **inaction**: no bond is forfeited, no claim is created, the proposer's position is preserved exactly, and withdrawal reopens on the roll that ends recovery. Relaxing `withdraw`'s epoch guard would be the *active* choice — releasing funds and mutating intake bookkeeping while the epoch clock is paused and carry-forward re-anchoring is mid-flight is more state movement during an incident, not less. Option (c) (re-anchoring carried-forward proposals at the *start* of recovery) was rejected for the same reason plus its interaction with the intake-queue admission bound. No code change.
