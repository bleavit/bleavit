## Question

**10 §9.2 sizes two device classes and states no rule for telling a client which one it is on.** The section publishes *"Hard caps: 300 MB desktop / 75 MB mobile"* and calls the shares *"fixed internal shares (user-adjustable locally)"*, and nothing anywhere says how a browser decides which cap applies. `platformBudget` therefore takes its platform as a **required argument with no default**, on the stated grounds that the unsafe direction is silently taking the desktop cap. The client now classifies from `navigator.userAgentData.mobile`, the one non-sniffing form-factor hint a browser publishes, and **fails closed to mobile** wherever it is absent — which today includes Firefox and Safari on the desktop, so a laptop on either takes the 75 MB cap and a permanently shallower history. That is the survivable direction under §9.2's *"degrades depth, never correctness"*, and it is a real product cost being paid by a rule the specification does not state. Two things need ruling: whether §9.2 should state the classification (and admit a third answer for *unknown*), and whether its *"user-adjustable locally"* clause reaches the **cap** as well as the shares — if it does, a desktop user misclassified as mobile has a remedy and the fallback is cheap; if it does not, the fallback is the whole story. Neighbouring row: SQ-763, on whether that same adjustability may reach a bound §9.3 states absolutely.
