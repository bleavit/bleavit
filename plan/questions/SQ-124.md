## Question

12 §6.3's keeper inactivity row ("no crank 1 h") doesn't say whether the signal is chain-observed (rebate/extrinsic events) or daemon-side, nor how to avoid false positives when a role legitimately has no due work for >1 h (no trading books d18–d21; capability-disabled roles on the current runtime). The reference keeper ships per-role metrics + last-success timestamps and the README recommends a `for: 1h` guard excluding disabled roles. Pin the intended semantics at the next 12 touch.

## Status

**resolved 2026-07-20 (B5 ratification; ratified as shipped, two claims corrected).** 12 §6.3 now fixes the reading normatively: the signal is **daemon-side**, evaluated per role against that role's last-successful-crank timestamp, deliberately not inferred from chain activity (which cannot tell an idle keeper from a role with no due work). The row's `for: 1h` recommendation is **not** what ships and was doubly wrong — `for:` is a Prometheus rule field, not an Alertmanager one, and the shipped rule uses no `for:` at all, filtering instead on `timestamp > 0 and planned_total > 0`. The spec ratifies the shipped filter, requires per-role instantiation so a cleanup role cannot mask a decision-critical one, and **declares both blind spots** the filter creates — a role stuck since process start (same zero timestamp as a disabled role) and, caught by the adversarial review, a keeper whose chain connection is down, which the liveness conjunct silences even though its cranks are equally not happening. `keeper/README.md` corrected to match.
