## Question

**15 §4.5 required the supply-chain gate to preserve cargo-audit's `unsound` informational classification, and the GitHub Advisory Database grades such advisories as vulnerabilities.** The section's two legs partition the space: cargo-audit gates everything RustSec covers and allows its informational kinds, while the pinned OSV leg gates *exactly the complement* and skips anything carrying a RUSTSEC id. RUSTSEC-2024-0429 (`glib` 0.18.5, unsound `VariantStrIter`) falls between them — GHSA-wrw7-89jp-8q8g grades it **medium**, Dependabot reported it against `app/Cargo.lock`, and `tools/ci/supply-chain-gates.sh` was green and would have stayed green forever. Neither leg is misconfigured. Should `unsound` remain informational because RustSec says so, or should a shipped artifact's exposure to the defect decide?

## Status

resolved 2026-08-11 (Dependabot triage): exposure decides. A fourth cargo leg (`tools/ci/check-unsound-advisories.py` + annotated `tools/ci/unsound-waivers.toml`) gates `unsound` alone — `unmaintained` names no defect or mechanism and stays an allowed warning, with the line held in one constant and one test. Waivers key on `(id, package, version, workspace)` and MUST declare an `exposure` of `unreachable`, `constrained` (affected function is called, so its call sites are cited) or `triggerable`; `triggerable` is refused at load time. 15 §4.5 amended in two places (the parenthetical and a new normative paragraph). No 02 change, so no `INTEGRATION_CONTRACT_VERSION` bump.
