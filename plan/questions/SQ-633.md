## Question

**Nothing in §8 says how much of a sampling round must be comparable before the client may call it evidence.** `effectiveCoverage` reported `meaningful`, true at or above a 50 % floor named in `LADDER`. §8.3 licenses the ladder's thresholds as **release constants** because *"a governance vote does not change how fast a third-party HTTP endpoint answers"* — that argument covers latency, a consecutive-failure count and a probe interval, all facts about networks, and it does not reach a line between weak and strong **evidence**, which is a claim about what the client knows. Nothing anchored 0.5: not a kernel constant, not a 13 §1 key, not published calibration. F9 deleted it rather than documenting it — the report is now three numbers (compared, sampled, ratio) and no verdict, so a screen states *"n of m rows were comparable"*, which is a fact. That is safe and it leaves a real gap: §8.4's own honest-guarantee copy is normative UI copy, and a client that must eventually say *"this round was weak evidence"* needs a number the specification names, together with its unsafe direction (too high suppresses honest rounds; too low dresses a two-row check as a hundred-row one)
