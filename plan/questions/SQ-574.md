## Question

**TH-72's attack-cost cell carries no figure, and the hosted service prices market depth at zero.** Found deriving N14's slot price, which needs a number to equalize against. Two halves. **(a) The spec half:** doc 14's TH-72 cost cell reads *"round-trip fees only — governance denial at a price that does not exist in the system today"*, and `threat_costs.py` prices exactly three rows (TH-11, TH-16, TH-64) — not this one. So the threat model cannot compare its own newest denial channel against the four it already prices in 08 §7. **(b) The code half, and it is the load-bearing one:** `register` admits `declared_stake = 1` base unit, the fee is `max(svc.fee_bps · stake, SVC_FEE_FLOOR_USDC)` so the flat 393-USDC floor binds, and `b` carries a **lower** bound only (`b >= b_min(stake, ε)`, ≈ 15 at that stake) with none above — so an attacker pays 393 USDC flat and posts **arbitrary** depth. Depth is what diverts flow; depth is free. **Derived in `reference-model/.../slot_pricing.py` (23 tests):** at equal harm (measured 0.50-rung formation loss, N12) the hosted door is cheaper than the cheapest 08 §7 channel on every class — PARAM 3.16×, TREASURY 2.79×, CODE 1.63×, META 1.23× — with per-epoch uplifts of 1,710 / 2,487 / 1,752 / 1,002 needed to reach parity. Compared at face value the spread reads 1.46×–8.08×, so **the harm normalization halves it** and the raw ratio would have overstated the hole by about two. The structural form: **the cheapest governance to deny is the class Bleavit subsidizes least**, because the attack cost tracks `pol.b` and PARAM's is smallest. It also verifies the Track N plan's unsourced ≈ 3,000 USDC/epoch estimate — 2,786 for CODE by an unrelated route, within 7 %. **Not exploitable today**: `svc.client_bond` is `[VERIFY]`-unset so `admit_client` refuses everyone and the service is inert — this is N14 scope, not an incident

## Status

**open — figures withdrawn 2026-08-03 by adversarial review; the code-verified half stands.** An independent read-only Codex pass (`gpt-5.6-luna`, `max`) was asked to refute five claims and refuted three. **(i) The depth arithmetic double-counted.** `pol.b` is quoted *per branch*, i.e. per book, and `parity_depth_cash` multiplied by a further 2 for "the question's two books" — every cash figure was 2× high. Verified independently before accepting. The error direction was **against this row's own conclusion**: it inflated the attacker's cost and understated the hole. It also regressed a figure already computed correctly by hand earlier the same day (1,589 for CODE), and it walked past the suite's only arithmetic guard, which pinned `b` versus `b·ln 2` and not the branch count. **(ii) The harm normalization smuggled an assumption**: every ratio divides by the hosted attack's measured harm while treating `intake_denial`'s harm as 1.0 — total denial — which is stated nowhere and justified nowhere, so each ratio is wrong by 1/H_I. **(iii) The capital charge is lower-order, not conservative.** Worst-case LMSR maker loss *is* the posted cash, ≈ 348× the carrying cost the model counted; excluding a term two orders of magnitude larger than the one included does not make a figure conservative, it makes it not a figure. **Consequently the uplift targets (1,710 / 2,487 / 1,752 / 1,002) and the ratios (3.16× / 2.79× / 1.63× / 1.23×) are WITHDRAWN and MUST NOT be carried into 13 or 16.** What survives is the code-verified half — a flat 393 USDC buys arbitrary depth — which rests on the registration path, not on any of the broken arithmetic. **Both remaining claims came back REFUTED (2026-08-03), and between them they take the row's headline and its anchor.** **(iv) 'A flat 393 USDC buys arbitrary depth' is false, and this row's own arithmetic contradicted it.** The *fee* is flat in depth — `declared_stake = 1` collapses the rate leg and the 393 floor binds however large `b` is — but depth is not bought with the fee: registration derives `escrow = 2·seed_headroom(b) = 2·b·ln 2`, the funder must hold it, and it is checked against `phase3.tvl_cap`, so depth tops out at `b ≈ 1,442,695` USDC (independently reproduced). The surviving statement is narrow: **the fee does not scale with depth; the capital requirement does**, and whether flat fees on TVL-bounded depth is itself a defect is now an open question rather than an asserted one. **(v) The equalization benchmark is invalid because the harms are incommensurable.** `intake_denial` fills the 64-entry queue so new submissions get `IntakeFull` — it blocks *admission* and cannot block the decision call for a proposal already in `Trading`, which is precisely what depth-thinning attacks. Crowding a queue and starving a live decision are different harms, so the cheaper is not a price for the other; `cheapest_priced_denial_channel` is **removed rather than re-pointed**, since `slot_capture` is closer in kind but is a different strategy at a different price and choosing between them needs evidence that does not exist. **Net: N14's case for demand-responsive pricing now rests entirely on *allocation* — sniping and first-come-first-served under contention, which needs no threat-cost benchmark — and the separate claim that hosted denial is *underpriced* is unsubstantiated and must not be asserted.** The module keeps the refutations rather than deleting them, because each is a way the service looked cheaper or more dangerous than it is. **The review's side finding is now SQ-575 and is larger than this row.** N14 owns the repair, and the repair is larger than the milestone assumed: occupancy-and-velocity pricing governs *allocation and sniping*, but one slot holds unlimited depth, so the fee also needs a **depth-proportional leg**. Two mechanisms, two jobs. Caveats travel with the figures: they rest on the proportional-to-depth flow model whose error direction 16 §8.4 records as unsafe (a better venue needs less depth ⇒ cheaper still), and they exclude the attacker's adverse-selection losses (⇒ dearer). Directional, not a bound either way
