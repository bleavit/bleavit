## Question

**A dispute whose money is already final still blocks every decision on its spec version, for up to 8 more days.** 07 §12's hold predicate is `Rounds::iter().any(...)` over component + merit floor, with no exclusion for a round whose money leg has been neutralized; the production `RuntimeEpochOracle::any_open_dispute_touching` matches it (`counter_value.is_some()` + bond ≥ floor, no `money_settled` check). But §11(1) **retains** a neutralized round until a terminal verdict disposes of its stack — up to the `OracleResolution` track's 7 d + 1 d — and I-18 guarantees the settled value can no longer change. So for that whole window the hold protects a quantity that is already decided, and every proposal whose frozen spec consumes the component takes `Reject(ProcessHold)`. Zero safety benefit, real liveness cost, and it is an attacker-selectable one: §11(1) already prices "ride a dispute to terminal to force neutral settlement" as a griefing move, and this makes the same purchase also buy an 8-day decision freeze on the component. The text predates retention (which arrived with A12), so §12 was written when a round at its deadline simply disappeared. Likely ruling: exclude money-settled rounds from the hold and say so in §12 — the contest that matters to `decide()` ends when the value does. Not folded into SQ-182 because it is a behavioural change to the decision path, not to the deadline. **Reachability changed with SQ-182**: before it, `force_neutralize_expired` had no caller, so scheduled d20 retention never happened in production.

## Status

**resolved** — liveness + griefing-price interaction. **Verified against HEAD 2026-07-26, and one of the row's own words is wrong in the safe-sounding direction.** The mechanism is exactly as described: `RuntimeEpochOracle::any_open_dispute_touching` (`runtime/bleavit-runtime/src/configs.rs:3856`) tests `counter_value.is_some()` + the `max(dis.merit_min, frozen B_1)` merit floor with **no** `money_settled` check, and a retained round satisfies both — `counter_value` is what *makes* it the retained case, and its round-cap bond is at or above `B_1` by construction. Nothing else bounds it: `stale_process_hold` is the dead-man/stale-epoch cutoff (`epoch-core:2144`), unrelated to disputes. **But it is not a \"freeze\".** `guards.process_hold` reaches `DecisionOutcome::Reject(RejectReason::ProcessHold)` (`epoch-core:1093`), which is T10/T20 → `ProposalState::Rejected` — **terminal**. Proposals reaching their decide window inside the window are not deferred until the dispute clears; they are rejected and must be resubmitted with a fresh bond, window and grace. The row's \"8-day decision freeze\" therefore understates the cost by a category, and the griefing purchase §11(4) prices as \"status quo\" silently also buys the death of every proposal consuming that component for the whole window. **Ruled 2026-07-26 (delegated): exclude money-settled rounds from §12's hold.** §12 holds a decision because \"a merit dispute on a consumed component contests the very quantity the proposal's scalar books will settle on\". Once the money leg is neutralized, I-18 makes that quantity final and a later verdict resolves bonds and reputation only — the contest that matters to `decide()` has ended, so the hold protects a decided number at the price of killing live proposals. The exclusion is per-round, not per-version, so a genuinely live round for another measurement epoch under the same spec still holds. It cannot be gamed in the permissive direction either: neutralization happens on the d20 schedule rather than at an attacker's choosing, and it sets the component to the neutral carry-last value, which is the conservative outcome. **Closed 2026-07-26** on `feat/sq494-money-settled-hold-scope`, and independently of #175: the test is the spec's own definition of non-money-bearing (§11(1) — \"a round whose `(component, epoch, spec_version)` already carries a settled `ComponentValues` entry\"), so the predicate reads `settled_component` and never touches the oracle's internal money-settled latch or its storage shape. Evaluated **after** the merit floor, so a sub-merit round never pays the read. **The row's real cost turned out to be a benchmark gap, not the predicate.** `decide`'s benchmark seeded **zero** `Rounds`, so §12's whole ProcessHold predicate — the 128-round scan, its per-match `RoundSchedules` read and the `dis.merit_min` param read — has never been charged on a decision-critical permissionless crank. Seeding it honestly moves `decide` 270 → 655 reads, and the +385 decomposes exactly: 128 scanned + 128 schedules + 128 component values + 1 param. **Only the 128 component-value reads are this change**; the other 257 are the pre-existing gap. The fixture seeds all 128 rounds on the proposal's own version. I first split it 64/64 on 02 §7.2's `16 × ≤4 × ≤2` decomposition and **that was wrong** — the review caught it. The 4-epoch factor holds only once a retained round is eventually reaped, and 07 §11(1)'s retention has no implemented deadline until SQ-492 (PR #175), so successive epochs accumulate money-settled rounds on one long-lived frozen version to the 128 cap. The two rows are independent *in code* and coupled *in the reachability argument*, which is exactly the coupling I had asserted did not exist. 527 → 655 reads; once #175 lands the fixture is merely conservative, the safe direction. The seeded rounds clear the merit floor **and** are money-settled, which is the true worst case: `.any()` short-circuits on the first holding dispute, so a map full of holding disputes is the *cheap* case. **Attempting the pre-SQ-494 baseline failed with `TooManyCohorts`** — without the exclusion every proposal is force-rejected into an already-saturated cohort set, which is the row's own defect refusing to let its benchmark run. **One 13 §5 derivation reopened:** the `decide` PoV pin (231,055 B, ceiling 384 KiB) is a deliberate drift detector and it fired. Re-derived to 404,514 B with a 512 KiB ceiling — 10.3 % of the 3,932,160 B normal-class budget beside `settle_cohort(5)`'s 9.8 %, so ~8× of headroom remains and the pin stays a detector. 13 §5 item 1 now names the round scan explicitly and states why its \"bounded regardless of map ceiling\" claim still holds: `MAX_ROUNDS` is a kernel constant, not a growing map.
