## Question

**Entrenched enactment is a fixed 84 days, not four live epochs.** `min_enactment_period = 4 × 21 d` while `epoch.length` is governable 14–42 d, so at maximum epoch length the priced-second-opinion window is ~2 epochs. Pre-dates B1b; stock `pallet-referenda::TrackInfo` cannot schedule relative to epoch boundaries — needs a specified custom enactment mechanism or a 06 §2.1 wording ruling.

## Status

**resolved 2026-07-26 — implemented as `4 × epoch.length`'s ceiling.** The 2026-07-21 ruling (four *actual* boundary crossings) stands; what changed is that the mechanics were verified, and they make the simplest implementation also the strongest. **Three facts settled the design.** (i) `pallet-referenda` reads the delay **once, at bake time** — `earliest_allowed = now + min_enactment_period` (vendored `schedule_enactment`) — so a delay derived from the *live* epoch length is still shaveable by a lengthening that lands after approval; `epoch.length`'s max-Δ 10 % / cooldown 2 epochs rate-limits that to roughly one boundary, not to zero. (ii) An exactly-four dispatch-time gate needs an approval-epoch anchor, and referenda exposes no on-confirm hook to record one — it would take a two-phase arm/apply surface across 06's authority matrix, new storage, and a scheduled dispatch that can fail and need re-attempt (a liveness surface) for the difference between "exactly 4" and "≥ 4". (iii) `epoch.length` is **kernel-bounded** (13 rule 7), so its whole metadata tuple — including the 42 d ceiling — is genesis-fixed and `amend_registry` refuses it. Sizing the delay at `4 × ceiling` (168 d) therefore guarantees ≥ 4 boundaries at **any** legal length, statically, with no new machinery: shortening epochs only adds boundaries. Sourced from `constitution_core::EPOCH_LENGTH_CEILING_BLOCKS`, the single home the registry ceiling is itself seeded from, and deliberately **not** the `fast-timing` arm — every 06 §2.1 track period stays at its release value in that build so governance windows cannot fire inside a minute-scale drill. **The cost is stated, not glossed:** at the 21 d default the delay doubles (84 → 168 d, ~210 d end to end), and it applies to every `entrenched`-class 13 §1 row in **both** directions — a *raise* of `att.bond` waits it out too. Accepted as the price of a guaranteed boundary count; a row whose adjustment latency outweighs its second opinion belongs in a different class, which is a 13-side decision. **Tests:** the track-table pin moves 84 → 168 and gains a derivation assertion (the delay must *equal* `4 × the ceiling constant`, not merely match a literal), plus a test reading `epoch.length`'s **live registry record** and asserting ≥ 4 boundaries at both its min and max — and that the row is `kernel_bounded`, since the whole guarantee rests on the ceiling being immutable.  **Codex review (PR #178) found three, all valid, all fixed or recorded.** (a) **P1 — the guarantee is conditional**: `epoch.tick` is permissionless, so elapsed blocks are not crossed epochs. The sizing is unchanged (nothing block-denominated can do better), but 06 §2.4 now states exactly what it delivers instead of claiming crossings, and the residual is **SQ-498** with its arm/apply design costed. My own sentence overclaimed; the reviewer was right to catch it. (b) **P1 — wrong home for the ceiling**: it went into `constitution-core` when `futarchy-primitives` already homes the matching *floor* (`PRODUCTION_MIN_EPOCH_LENGTH_BLOCKS`) — the symmetry I had noted in my own research and then ignored. Now `kernel::PRODUCTION_MAX_EPOCH_LENGTH_BLOCKS`, with the registry seed and the runtime both deriving from it and no runtime dependency on the constitution pallet for a kernel value. (c) **P2 — the exact-ratio assertion fails under `fast-timing`**, where the registry ceiling compresses while the delay deliberately does not; the cross-profile invariant is `4 × max <= delay`, with equality asserted only on the production profile.
