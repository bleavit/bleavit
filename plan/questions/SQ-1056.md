## Question

*(Raised as SQ-1048 on 2026-08-10 and renumbered on 2026-08-11: #300 minted the same id independently for the supply-chain `unsound` gate, and merged to `main` first. That row keeps the id, because it is cited in normative spec text — [15](../../docs/architecture/15-invariants-and-testing.md) §4.5 twice — and in AGENTS.md, while this one was cited once, in 08 §2.6's own prose. This is the collision `check-spec-question-batches.py` was written to catch, and it caught it.)* **08 §2.6 rule 2 does not say whether a sale's "proceeds" is gross or net of `mkt.fee`.** Rule 1 spells its arm out as `cost + fee`, and rule 2 says only "adds proceeds". The two readings differ by the fee on **every** sale, and the word is load-bearing: net is what a trader actually received, gross credits them value the protocol took. TR4 implemented the net reading and its review judged gross "a defect", so the implementation is not in doubt — the **specification text** is, and rule 1's explicitness is what makes rule 2's silence read as deliberate rather than elided.

## Status

resolved 2026-08-10 — **net**, and rule 2 now says so: `proceeds − fee`. Net is what the seller actually received, and the book withholds exactly `fee_up(proceeds, mkt.fee)` out of the gross figure the `Traded` event carries. Crediting gross would score a trader on value the protocol took, which is the one rounding direction R-7 forbids. It also restores the symmetry rule 1 already had: a buy is charged **more** than the book cost and a sale is credited **less**, so the fee is claimant-adverse on both sides and a round trip at an unchanged price scores strictly negative. TR4's implementation was already net and is unchanged; this closes the text, not the code
