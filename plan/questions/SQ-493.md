## Question

**07 §10's two-consecutive-flag renormalization is unimplemented: nothing reads `SettledComponent.flagged`.** §10 is explicit — "two consecutive flagged epochs ⇒ affected not-yet-settled cohorts recompute `W` without the component, weights renormalized" — and assigns it to welfare. No reader of `flagged` existed in `pallets/welfare` or `crates/welfare-core`; the oracle wrote the field and nobody consumed it, so a component that failed twice running still entered `W` at its stale carried value with full weight — precisely the case §10 says must be dropped. Reachable in practice since SQ-182 gave the flag-writing deadline crank its first production caller.

## Status

**resolved 2026-07-26 — implemented and differentially verified.** **Design (ruled 2026-07-26, delegated).** The flagged set is recorded **when observed**, not re-derived: `compute_settlement` read only the snapshot's already-computed scalar `welfare`, and SQ-492's epoch cutoff makes the oracle's flag history legitimately absent by settlement time. A pallet-internal `SnapshotContexts: map (EpochId, MetricSpecVersion) → {flagged, incident_multiplier, params}` sits beside `Snapshots`, one per snapshot, pruned in lockstep and paired **in both directions** by try-state. The `baseline_carry` precedent (`epoch-core:2005`), and SQ-491's "it has to be a latch" written out one more time. **`incident_multiplier` and the tunables are stored with it** because the recompute must differ from the recorded `W` in the drop alone — recovering `I_e` as `c_settlement ÷ c_joint` is lossy in an unaudited direction, and re-reading live `Params` would silently re-price an already-measured epoch. **Semantics, now normative in 07 §10 (five points) and 05 §4.4 (the arithmetic).** Only attested components can be flagged and welfare **refuses** a snapshot offering any other (§11(1)(i)); consecutiveness is evaluated over the cohort's own window — flagged at `(e, e+1)` or `(e+1, e+2)` — and applied to *both* its epochs, so two cohorts sharing a measured epoch may weigh it differently, which is why §10 makes this settlement-time and not a property of the published `W`; an absent record is **not** a flagged epoch (a version's first live epoch has no predecessor, and §10 grants the first flagged epoch its carried value anyway). **Which groups may be emptied is asymmetric, and that was the substantive ruling.** `S` and `C` enter `W` only through `g(S)`, `g(C)`: a gate has no weight to renormalize away, and both a `min` over nothing and a weighted product over nothing evaluate to the *most favourable* value 1 — so an emptied gate group would convert total unavailability into a perfect score. Those decline the drop; gate-input failure stays VOID's business (05 §4.7). `P`/`A` are terms of one composite, so an emptied group renormalizes one level up and a dark `A` leaves `W = g(S)·g(C)·P` — measured on what could be measured, never on a fabricated `A = 1`. **No contract bump, for a stronger reason than the ruling had:** 02 §6 states that `pallet-welfare` has **no** event in the ingest set and that adding an off-contract diagnostic moves no version, so even the new `SettlementRenormalized` event was free. Contract stays **v15**. **Verification:** the reference model implemented the same rule independently from spec text alone (rule 1, `.claude/rules/reference-model.md`) and the corpus grew four `welfare_scenarios` rows — including a **controlled A/B pair at live gates**, identical inputs differing in the drop alone — which `welfare-core` replays **bit-exactly** through the production settlement path (`W' = 0.428893718` for the emptied-A row against `0.416703840` un-dropped). Plus 10 core tests (streak-before-window, the consecutive-cohorts divergence, emptied-A, the declined gate group, per-epoch tunables, attested-only, prune pairing, the post-amendment liveness case) and 3 pallet wiring tests.
