## Question

**May normative UI copy be a plain-English rendering rather than a quotation, and what binds the rendering when it is?** 10 §8.4 designates its honest-guarantee statement *"normative UI copy"* and 11 §11.2/§10.4 require *fixed* copy per surface and per code, but no section says whether *fixed* means **verbatim**. This client renders §8.4's sentence in plainer English (*"a source that has stopped responding"* for *"catch liveness failures"*), which is defensible for the audience and has now drifted **twice** in the load-bearing nouns — `labels` for `recommends`, then `sources` for `snapshot producers`, the second naming a cross-check `FE-PROV-004` does not implement. Both survived a clause-by-clause binding because the binding paired the section's clause with the substituted rendering. A verbatim rule needs no binding at all and is trivially checkable; a rendering rule needs one, and needs it in **both** directions, since a doc→constant table cannot see a substitution inside the constant. **Ruling needed:** is normative UI copy quoted verbatim, or rendered under a stated binding obligation — and if rendered, does the obligation belong in 10 §10.4 (the per-code copy taxonomy) or in 15 §4.8 (as a required frontend suite row)?
