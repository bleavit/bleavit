## Question

**The partition bounds dispatch, not transport, so a client's XCM traffic still moves `H`.** Found by the fourth adversarial review of the 16 §8.5 partition (PR #221) and confirmed by reading the runtime: message-queue servicing, decode, barrier evaluation and origin conversion run as `Mandatory` `on_initialize` work attached to no top-level dispatch, so §8.5's own residual fold attributes them to `PrimaryUsed`. Two scenarios, the second sharper: a valid ingress template charges its **call body** to `external_used` while its transport overhead lands in primary; and a message whose call the `ExternalClient` safe-call filter refuses consumes primary queue weight with **zero** external reservation, because the resource dispatcher is never reached. That makes the amended §8.5 and 15's PT-10 mutually inconsistent as first written — one says residual work is primary, the other says arbitrary service traffic leaves welfare byte-identical. Three things bound it and none closes it: the exposure is capped by the queue's `ServiceWeight`; `H` clamps to 1 below the 40 % utilization target, so transport must first push utilization past it; and inbound execution is **priced** through the 09 §6 trader at `xcm.usdc_per_sec`, so this is not the unpriced channel I-24 forbids. Closing it properly needs per-message domain attribution of queue servicing, which `pallet-message-queue` does not expose

## Status

**open.** Interim ruling (R-1, mine): PT-10's byte-identical property is **scoped to dispatch-attributable** service work and the transport residual is stated explicitly in both 16 §8.5 and 15 PT-10 rather than left implicit — an honest boundary beats an inconsistent claim. Not a merge blocker for #221: the residual is pre-existing, and #221 strictly narrows the primary side rather than widening it
