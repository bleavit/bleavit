## Question

**The trading reward program may authorize the *whole* Phase 3–4 incentive pot, while [08](../../docs/architecture/08-treasury-and-economics.md) §2.1 calls its claim a "share".** §2.1's row assigns the 100,000,000 VIT pot to *"Trading/keeper/reporter bootstrap incentives"* and says it *"backstops the reporter loans of §2.5"*, then that *"§2.6 delivers the trading **share**"*. §2.6 in turn bounds a funding call by *"its stored remaining allocation"*, and the runtime binds that to the full genesis allocation — so one `FutarchyParam` decision can move the entire pot into the trading sovereign. **No allocation table splits the 10% three ways, and inventing one would be R-2 fabrication.** The other two named uses have no delivery mechanism at all today, so there is nothing to protect them and nothing to size them from. The §2.5 backstop is the one quantified claim — a **225,000–375,000 USDC** line at 3–5 reporters × 75,000 — but it is denominated in USDC against a VIT pot, and a reserve computed through the governed rate is the reflexivity D-18 keeps out of every sizing formula. **The error direction is unsafe and it is not merely economic:** Phase-3 arming requires three reporters at full stake, the treasury's recallable loans are what bootstrap them, and that line is backstopped by this pot. An unfunded backstop is a protocol that cannot arm. Two things bound the live exposure rather than close the question. The 2026-08-10 ruling folds the return of unspent budget into the funding call, so an over-authorization is recoverable except for what has actually been paid. And payout is rate-limited by construction — at most `snapshot_bond / rate_headroom` per participant per epoch across at most `MaxParticipants` accounts. So this is a sizing judgement, not a live defect. **R-2's escalation clause applies exactly:** a value whose error direction is unsafe and which no evidence in this repository anchors. Either §2.1's "share" is loose wording that should be corrected to say the pot is one undivided allocation, or the values layer owes a sub-allocation that only the owner can set.

## Status

resolved 2026-08-11 — **the whole pot, and the owner ruled it.** The first branch: §2.1's allocation is one undivided 100,000,000 VIT block, no table splits it three ways, and §2.6 MAY authorize all of it. The runtime already bound exactly that — `IncentiveAllocationAmount` is the full genesis allocation and TR6's migration seeds `IncentiveRemaining` to it — so the ruling confirms the code and changes none of it. No file under `app/`, `tools/`, `crates/`, `pallets/` or `runtime/` cites this id, so no gate expires with it. Three spec edits carry the ruling: §2.1's row now says the allocation is undivided and retires the word *"share"*, §2.6's opening says the subsection may draw the whole pot, and **§2.5 now states the reporter-loan backstop as subordinate** — what stands behind that USDC line is the part of the pot §2.6 has not authorized, and nothing reserves a floor for it. That last edit is the substantive consequence rather than wording: Phase-3 arming needs three reporters this line bootstraps, so sequencing the line **before** a large authorization became an operational requirement, and §2.6's return of unspent budget makes an over-authorization recoverable down to what has actually been paid out. One question the ruling did not reach was **decided here rather than deferred**: `fund_trading_rewards` is a PARAM leaf, and a PARAM decision moving 10 % of supply looks disproportionate until one notices §1.4's `create_community_schedule` is the same shape over a 25 % allocation, which §2.6 cites as its own precedent. The spec is internally consistent, so the precedent governs and no new question is raised
