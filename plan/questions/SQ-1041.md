## Question

**No document states an accessibility or contrast target for the canonical client, so "non-suppressible" has no measurable meaning.** 10 §2.1 requires provenance labelling to be *"mandatory, non-suppressible"* and INV-FE-9 admits *"no unlabeled rendering path"*, but neither says whether a label a user cannot read satisfies them, and 15 §4.8's layer table has no accessibility row. The F28 review found five dark-theme pairs under 3:1 — three of them provenance badges — and had to argue them from the *intent* of "non-suppressible" rather than measure them against a rule; they are fixed, but the next such regression has nothing to fail against. Two sub-questions the same ruling should settle: **(a)** should 10 or 15 name a target (WCAG 2.2 AA for text and non-text contrast is the obvious candidate) and back it with a gate, and **(b)** 15 §6's INV-FE-9 row already names *"visual regression"* as a certifying test while §4.8's layer table has no row for it — is that mention normative, in which case §4.8 owes it a layer, or descriptive? F28 is the first change that makes visual regression meaningful and it ships none.
