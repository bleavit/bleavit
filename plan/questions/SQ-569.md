## Question

**Any signed account can exhaust the external quota with calls it is not authorized to make.** Raised by the fourth compliance review of the 16 §8.5 partition (PR #221) and left unrecorded until now. The partition classifies by **resource domain**, so a hosted call is external whoever signs it — that is deliberate and it is what closed the laundering hole. The consequence was not written down: an unregistered signer can submit, say, `ClientRegistry::top_up_delivery_float`, have the full charge reserved against `external_used` in `validate`/`prepare`, fail origin validation at dispatch, and — per §8.5's explicit rule that *a failed call retains the complete reservation* — keep the reservation. Repeated once per block it exhausts the external side and makes legitimate client calls fail `ExhaustsResources`. **The asymmetry is the point:** external capacity is `Operational.reserved`, deliberately a small slice of the block, so denying paying clients costs a fraction of what denying the chain would. Bleavit's own governance is untouched, which is why this is availability rather than solvency, and the attacker pays a full transaction fee per attempt

## Status

**open.** The reviewer classified it as an availability consequence rather than a §8.5 divergence, and I agree it is not a merge blocker for #221 — the retain-on-failure rule is correct under R-7 (a free failure is a free DoS on the accounting itself) and the exposure is one block wide. What is wrong is that no document says the vector exists. Two candidate closures, neither free: a pre-dispatch registered-client check in `validate` for the calls that *require* a client origin — which cannot be blanket, because `Market::buy` on an external book is legitimately open to any trader, so it would duplicate authority logic in a transaction extension and drift; or accept it and price it, with a doc-14 threat row and a 12 §6.3 alert on external-quota exhaustion. Prefer the second unless measurement shows the first is needed
