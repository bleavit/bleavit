## Question

**What is the reserve-health metric `R` on a chain where no probe can be sent or answered?** 07 §8's scoring says "no probe sent (keeper outage) ⇒ probe **fail**. Absence is never healthy", while §8(3) makes recovery depend on `res.recover_threshold` consecutive **passes**. Neither contemplates SQ-380's situation, where the dispatcher and the response router are absent *by construction*, so fails accrue and passes are unreachable — the scoring rule and the recovery guarantee contradict each other. Two readings: **(a) literal** — absence is a fail, so the chain is correctly `ReserveUnhealthy` and 08 §1.2 should freeze spending until the probe feed ships; **(b) not-measuring** — the scoring rule presumes a working probe and a structurally absent one should advance no state at all (implementation: gate `crank_reserve_probe`'s state advance on `ProbeDispatch::live()`). Reading (b) is what makes SQ-205's sink safe to bind live. This must be answered before SQ-380 can close either way.

## Status

**resolved 2026-07-24 (answered by the 07 §8 amendment)** — neither reading (a) nor (b) governs: 07 §8 now specifies a third, and the implementation matches it. The probe begins **unarmed** and scores zero pre-arm slots (time before a complete runnable probe existed is not retroactively an outage); the first readiness-qualified crank sets the monotone `ReserveProbeArmed` latch; every completed slot thereafter is fail-static per the literal rule. A runtime with no live dispatcher or authenticated response handler is **release-invalid**, not "not measuring". SQ-380 and SQ-205 are consequently unblocked and also closed.
