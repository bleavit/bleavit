## Question

**The client bond is native VIT, but I-36 spends it on XCM delivery fees — and nothing says how one becomes the other.** Surfaced by N4's implementation, which followed the B19 (`pallet-attestor`) native-custody precedent my own brief pointed it at, and correctly so: a registration bond is a governance-adjacent security deposit, which is what B19 holds natively. The consequence was thought through by neither of us. [16](../../docs/architecture/16-hosted-question-service.md) §9 / I-36 make the bond the **source of prepaid egress delivery fees**, and XCM delivery fees are paid in an asset the router accepts — DOT or USDC under 09 §6.1's trader, never VIT. So "prepaid from the client's bond" currently names a conversion that does not exist, at a price nothing publishes. The precedent is genuinely mixed rather than obviously one way: `att.bond` is native VIT, while `orc.reporter_stake` is **100,000 USDC**

## Status

**resolved 2026-08-01 (N7 prep).** Ruled **(b)**: the bond stays native VIT on the B19 discipline and a **separate USDC `delivery_float`** pays for egress. Two balances are more to reason about than one, and that cost is accepted for one specific reason — it is the only option that introduces **no price**. (a) all-USDC discards the B19 hold discipline for a deposit that is governance-adjacent by nature; (c) charging VIT at a governed rate puts a VIT/USDC rate on a **fee path**, and 08 §9's placeholder rate is explicitly not fit for that. The failure mode is bounded and lands where it already was allowed to: an exhausted float stops **pushes only**, and §9 already makes pull the authoritative delivery and push best-effort. Landed in 16 §2 (the `delivery_float` row + the ruling), 16 §9 and 15 I-36. **Unblocks N9**
