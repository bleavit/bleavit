The leg simply stopped at storage. **The three surfaces fail differently, and only one of them fails safe.** An **error** is fail-safe but misdiagnosing: a client decoding `DispatchError::Module { index, error }` against a feed missing that variant cannot name it, so a precise refusal renders through INV-FE-12's raw-bytes path and the user is told less than the chain said. A **call** is the sharpest, because `packages/papi-descriptors` is generated from this blob — a call absent from the feed is one the transaction builder cannot construct, so a whole workflow is silently missing rather than visibly broken. An **event** is V-164's exact shape: `event-accounts.ts` resolves accounts by metadata **type id**, so an event the feed omits is one `local-index` cannot ingest, presenting as an **empty transaction history** with no error. **What the ruling owes, beyond "add the check".** The expensive release leg rebuilds and byte-diffs, so it catches all three by construction, and the cheap leg's own docstring claims it *"would have caught the live defect on day one, by all four of its checks independently"*. Does that commit the per-commit leg to a **complete** in-repo surface comparison, or is it a cheap sample the release leg backs? Doc 15 §4.5 says neither today. **One caveat the ruling must price in, because it is not obvious:** errors and events are plain enums and the source comparison is exact on this tree — 426 and 238, with the only two disagreements the genuine ones — while **calls are not**. A `#[pallet::call_index]` → `pub fn` scan over a 400-character window finds **4 of `pallet-market`'s 7**, and the three it misses are **`buy` (+490 chars), `sell` (+817) and `sweep_revenue` (+433)** — long weight expressions and doc comments push the name out of the window, so the scan is blind on precisely the two calls the whole client trades through. Measured, and it is worse than no leg: the miss is one-directional, so the gate reports nothing and reads as coverage. Any extension stays **one-directional and in-repo only**, exactly as the storage leg is and for the same reason — most pallets here are SDK pallets whose source is not in this tree. Raised on 2026-08-11 by the re-record itself, which is why the drift is closed while the gate hole is not.

## Question

**`check-chain-feed.py` compares which pallets exist and what storage they publish, and compares no error, event or call variant at all.** So the feed can go stale in **426 error variants, 238 events and every call** across the 18 in-repo pallets, under a gate written to catch a stale feed. Measured on this branch rather than argued: the feed was correctly re-recorded at `6eb62b51`, then TR9 added `Constitution::RewardRateAboveWashBreakeven` at `00a5b562` and the final review's I5 added `TradingRewards::ThirdPartyWouldClampReward` at `d887b3e5`. Both feed directories went stale by exactly those two variants and the gate stayed green. **This is [SQ-582](#spec-questions) one layer over, and that row's own argument covers it.** SQ-582 added the storage leg because *"the pallet-set check compares which pallets exist and never what they publish"*. An error is also something a pallet publishes. The leg simply stopped at storage. **The three surfaces fail differently, and only one of them fails safe.** An **error** is fail-safe but misdiagnosing: a client decoding `DispatchError::Module { index, error }` against a feed missing that variant cannot name it, so a precise refusal renders through INV-FE-12's raw-bytes path and the user is told less than the chain said. A **call** is the sharpest, because `packages/papi-descriptors` is generated from this blob — a call absent from the feed is one the transaction builder cannot construct, so a whole workflow is silently missing rather than visibly broken. An **event** is V-164's exact shape: `event-accounts.ts` resolves accounts by metadata **type id**, so an event the feed omits is one `local-index` cannot ingest, presenting as an **empty transaction history** with no error. **What the ruling owes, beyond "add the check".** The expensive release leg rebuilds and byte-diffs, so it catches all three by construction, and the cheap leg's own docstring claims it *"would have caught the live defect on day one, by all four of its checks independently"*. Does that commit the per-commit leg to a **complete** in-repo surface comparison, or is it a cheap sample the release leg backs? Doc 15 §4.5 says neither today. **One caveat the ruling must price in, because it is not obvious:** errors and events are plain enums and the source comparison is exact on this tree — 426 and 238, with the only two disagreements the genuine ones — while **calls are not**. A `#[pallet::call_index]` → `pub fn` scan over a 400-character window finds **4 of `pallet-market`'s 7**, and the three it misses are **`buy` (+490 chars), `sell` (+817) and `sweep_revenue` (+433)** — long weight expressions and doc comments push the name out of the window, so the scan is blind on precisely the two calls the whole client trades through. Measured, and it is worse than no leg: the miss is one-directional, so the gate reports nothing and reads as coverage. Any extension stays **one-directional and in-repo only**, exactly as the storage leg is and for the same reason — most pallets here are SDK pallets whose source is not in this tree. Raised on 2026-08-11 by the re-record itself, which is why the drift is closed while the gate hole is not.
