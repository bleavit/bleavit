## Question

**11 §11.5's P-13 requires a reporter to hold the *full* stake and the runtime does not check it, so the canonical client refuses what the chain accepts — and the two documents disagree about whether it should.** P-13 reads *"caller in reporter registry with full `ReporterStake` held"*; `oracle_core::Oracle::report` checks `is_reporter` (`NotRegistered`) and never compares the held stake. **07 §2(5) is the other side and it is explicit**: a reporter slashed to half stake on a second adjudicated-false report *"remains registered until the third"*, and the stated consequence is narrower than P-13's — the `orc.n_min` count *"MUST exclude any seat holding less than `orc.reporter_stake`"*, which is loss of **countability**, not loss of the ability to report. `register_reporter` re-seats such an account at half stake deliberately, and **no top-up dispatch exists**, so reading P-13 as a dispatch precondition would make the half-stake state a permanent silent ejection and render 07 §2(5)'s *"until the third"* unreachable — there can be no third adjudicated-false report from an account that cannot report. That is self-contradictory, which is the argument that the **client precondition is the wrong side**, not the runtime. This is SQ-552's class exactly — a client refusing an action the runtime would accept — and `check-dispatch-mirror.py` did not catch it because P-13's clause is prose in §11.5 with no counterpart declared in 09 §1.2's backend list

## Status

**open — raised by Codex review on PR #248, verified in `oracle-core` (`report` at the `is_reporter` ensure; the half-stake re-seat in `register_reporter`).** Codex proposed adding the runtime check; the analysis above says the opposite, and the direction matters because one option amends audit-scope oracle code while the other amends a client precondition. **Not ruled here** — it turns on whether 07 §2(5) intends a half-stake reporter to keep reporting, which is a values/mechanism question about the offense ladder rather than an implementation detail, and getting it backwards either bricks a seat governance meant to keep or lets an under-collateralized reporter post. The client's block stands unchanged meanwhile: it is the conservative direction and refuses nothing the chain needs. Filed to **X** (real code)
