## Question

**A lawful release-key revocation raises a page-severity false alarm, and the frozen layout cannot express what 12 §1.3 requires.** (a) Executing 12 §2.3 literally — bump `keyring_generation`, set the bit, publish the new keyring "via the next release" — drives the shipped `attestation_monitor.py` to `ok = False` on `release/keyring/ReleaseChannel generation mismatch` for the entire window between step 1 and step 3, at **0 byte mismatches and 0 resolver divergences**, i.e. against neither of the rule's own declared thresholds. That fires `BleavitReleaseIntegrity`, **`severity: page`, `RB-RELEASE`** — the only channel that reaches pinned and stranded apps. (b) 02 §12's fixed 168-byte layout carries one generation field and an 8-byte mask over "key indices within the generation's published keyring", so the bit addresses the **new** generation's keyring: a key compromised at generation 7 is modelled as **not refused** at generations 8–11, while each replacement at the same index is. 12 §1.3's MUST is therefore unimplementable for historical releases, and 64 is a *per-generation* capacity that becomes a lifetime cap only under an undocumented re-listing workaround. **Ruling needed:** the revocation encoding across a generation bump, and the monitor's tolerance during a lawful revocation window
