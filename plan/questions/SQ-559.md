## Question

**The flat D-1 VOID schedule puts a price floor under every leg, and under asymmetric branch weights it can price a true REJECT as an ADOPT.** With `w_accept ≠ w_reject` the contamination stops being status-quo-favouring: at π = 0.02 and `w_accept` = 0.2, true decision values 0.400/0.364 give a true uplift of 0.036 (REJECT) while the contaminated series prices 0.0391415 (ADOPT), with **both gate vetoes clear** — a continuous vulnerable interval of roughly `w_accept ∈ [0.196, 0.288]`. As `w → 0` every leg approaches exactly 0.5 independent of truth. 04 §12's own worked ADOPT flips at π = 23/398 ≈ 0.0578 (the trailing hurdle, earlier than the full-window equality at 7/82) — safe direction, toward status quo. The audit's counterexample was **unlawful** (branch weights not summing to 1) and its near-degenerate witness would have been stopped by the gate vetoes; the corrected witness clears both. Note the Baseline leg is contaminated by a *different* mechanism (05 §7's neutral settlement), not by D-1. **Ruling needed:** whether the decision rule must read a VOID-adjusted series — and, first, whether the architecture adopts any risk-neutral pricing premise at all. **CORRECTED 2026-08-01 by adversarial review of PR #206, before any ruling was taken.** The contamination result is **conditional, not a mechanical contradiction**: D-1 defines terminal claim values and nowhere states that an LMSR quote equals expected payout over expected branch-USDC value, nor defines `π`, `w`, or convergence to that quotient — on-chain prices are inventory-derived. The `π`/`w` model is now explicitly non-normative sensitivity analysis and reports informationally rather than as a defect
