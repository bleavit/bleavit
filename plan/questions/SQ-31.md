## Question

06 §3.3's `MAX_NESTED` "≤ 16 calls total" bound is ambiguous in scope. The prose + 13 §2 read as a **global** "≤ 16 calls total" ("matching the payload bounds" = `prop.max_calls` = 16), but the §3.3 reference pseudocode checks `calls.len() <= MAX_NESTED_TOTAL` **per `batch`** (direct children of one batch; no count applied to non-batch wrappers). `origins-core`'s `SafetyFilter` implements a single **global** budget in which *every* node — each wrapper **and** each leaf — consumes one, so a flat `batch` of 16 public leaves (17 nodes) is rejected `TooManyCalls` even though its 16 leaf-calls equal `prop.max_calls`. This decides whether a maximum-size committed payload can be enacted as one batch through the execution guard's execute-time `SafetyFilter` re-check (I-11; A11/B1a). The core's global-node reading is the conservative, G-1-safe one but may over-reject a legitimate maximal batch. Which is normative — global-node count, global-leaf count, or per-batch direct-children?

## Status

**resolved 2026-07-20 (batch B2 · ratified).** The global-node reading is normative: 06 §3.3 gained the counting rule — every node, wrapper and leaf alike, costs one unit of a **single budget shared by the whole evaluation**, never reset per wrapper — and its reference pseudocode was corrected from the per-`batch` form that created the ambiguity (that form bounds no total, so it cannot deliver §3.3's own bounded-weight guarantee); 13 §2's `MAX_NESTED` row carries the note. **The row's stated rationale was refused, not ratified:** the guard payload is a top-level `BoundedVec` of `RuntimeCall` bounded by `prop.max_calls` (09 §1.2(11)), *not* a `batch`, so a maximal 16-call payload costs exactly 16 units and executes — verified symmetric at screening (`classifier.rs:103`) and execute (`execution-guard/src/lib.rs:1233`). There is no liveness trap. The only observable cost is user-facing: a directly submitted `utility.batch` admits 15 children, not 16.
