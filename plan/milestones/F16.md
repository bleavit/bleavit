**Started 2026-08-05; the G-1…G-9 precondition rows are in.** The spec-first check came back clean (V-113): all nine §11.7.2 reads are frozen in 02 §7.6, carried in the manifest and probed in `CRITICAL_SURFACE`, so there is no SQ-580-class gap here — worth stating because a first grep said the opposite and nearly became a false spec question. **Two subjects are the whole content of the slice.** `G-5 unlock(class, target)` reads **`recipient`**, not the caller: pallet-conviction-voting unlocks for an account anyone may name, so reading the caller's locks passes green whenever the caller has none — the ordinary case when somebody unlocks for a friend — and the chain then refuses. That is P-9's finding in a new place: a third account is a third subject, not a special case of the first two. And `G-2`'s delegation target is a **chain-read identity** (§11.3 anti-substitution), not an echo of the form. Everything else on the conviction-voting rows is `acting` rather than `signer`, because under a proxy the votes and locks are the proxied account's while the fee and nonce stay with the signer — with G-6/G-7's deposits genuinely `signer`, so the distinction is not vacuous. The **conviction-lock consequence** is marked `aboveTheFold` on G-1 and G-2 in the row itself rather than left to a screen, since 11 §11.7.6 and §11.2 constraint 3 both name it and a screen is where it gets forgotten. **What is deliberately absent:** no clause asserts a referendum will pass, and G-6's prospective `referendum_index` is a **warning, not a precondition** — the index is decided by inclusion order and no read at B′ can bind it, so encoding it would block a lawful submission on a race the user cannot avoid. 7 mutations, 0 survived; one showed the surface binding is enforced by the **compiler** (`SurfaceId` is a union generated from `CRITICAL_SURFACE`), and the suite says so rather than taking credit. **S9/S10's read screens landed the same session**, and §11.7.6's three statements are made **structural** rather than remembered. A tally's sides are `Verified<bigint>` **individually**, not one `Verified<Tally>`: wrapping the pair would let a single status stand for both and hide a provenance mismatch between them. `Confirming` is its **own arm** of the status union carrying the abort copy, so a screen cannot render it without saying that the countdown is not a countdown — the state where a user most needs telling. And `ReferendumCall`'s `undecodable` arm has **no name field**, so 10 §5.4's *"never guessed"* is a thing a screen cannot do rather than a rule it must remember. Three of the five mutations were caught by the **compiler** for that reason: removing a narrowing makes the field access untypeable. **One survived and was mine** — the mixed-provenance test drove only the detail view, so a list rendering `ayes` in both columns passed; a tally whose two columns show the same number is the one rendering error a reader cannot detect from the screen. **S11 landed too, and its rule is the one with no on-chain symptom.** §11.7.5(3): a user whose locks **post-date the snapshot** can sign a vote that is *weightless* — the extrinsic succeeds, they believe they voted, and nothing afterwards tells them otherwise, so the entire obligation is pre-sign. `EffectivePower` is a **union, not a number plus a flag**, because a number plus a flag renders without the flag: the `weightless` arm carries **no power field**, so there is no zero to misread. And §11.7.5 rule 2 still carries an open **`[VERIFY]` on the snapshot mechanism**, so there is a third arm — `unestablished` — which is what the client uses until that lands and which also has no power field. R-2's rule made structural: a voting power computed from a guessed snapshot rule would look exactly like one that was checked. Rule 4's *not-a-routine-vote* copy is unconditional and asserted across all three power states, so it cannot become conditional on having weight. 5 mutations, 0 survived — the weightless-renders-a-zero one caught by the **compiler**, since the arm genuinely has no field to read. **§11.7.4's ratification panel landed too, and its rule is the sharpest in the section.** *"The guard status is not the referendum lifecycle source. `NoPassedRecord` … deliberately does not distinguish never submitted, submitted-but-unbound, submitted and ongoing, or submitted and rejected."* A panel rendering that record as *"not ratified"* lies in the most consequential direction available on the screen — the referendum may be ongoing and about to pass, and a proposer reading *not ratified* concludes there is nothing to wait for. So `RatificationView`'s `no-passed-record` arm **requires** the referendum link: a caller holding only the guard's record cannot construct the view, and the rule is a **missing argument** rather than something to remember. The cannot-complete warning is **arithmetic** (remaining periods vs blocks to `grace_end`) and returns `undefined` — not `false` — for a referendum that is not ongoing, because a proposal nobody has submitted a referendum for has no remaining-period figure and a confident answer there would be to a different question. The warning says the proposal *will* reject, so a false positive tells a proposer to abandon something still live; the boundary is tested at exactly-fits. The window renders **block numbers as well as** a countdown, since a countdown alone presents an estimate as the deadline and is wrong in the direction that runs out of time. 6 mutations, 0 survived, two caught by the compiler. **The conviction model closed the last of F16's non-form work.** §11.7.1 requires the lock duration *"displayed **before** signing"*, and §11.2 constraint 3 names `conviction-vote-lock` among the five facts that may not sit behind a step — so `LockDisclosed` is **branded**, `discloseLock` is its only producer, and `VoteForSigning` requires one. A vote that reached a signer with no lock ever computed **does not typecheck**, and the negative-compilation corpus carries the forged disclosure. Same device as `GatePassed`, same reason: the failure is silent — the extrinsic succeeds and the user learns up to **32 enactment periods** later. The multipliers were **read from the pinned SDK** (`polkadot-stable2606`, `conviction.rs`: 0/1/2/4/8/16/32) rather than inferred from the doubling, because the pattern *looks* like it starts at 1 and these numbers are what a user's tokens are locked by; they are wire format and compiled in, while the **enactment period** they multiply is chain state and is an argument with no default. `None` is modelled as a **real conviction** — it votes at 10 % and locks nothing — since treating it as *not chosen* would let an unset field silently mean *no lock*. And `acceptsConviction` exists because a split vote that appeared to take 6× would tell the user both that their tokens are locked and that their vote weighs 6×, and neither is true. 6 mutations, 0 survived. **The forms closed it.** Three controls, because the model layer already carries the rest.
